\begin{abstract}
We investigate exact optimal binary search trees with ordered weights on
both keys and gaps. For alternating nonnegative gap weights, a leaf-deletion
argument gives a global gap-cost lower bound attained by every
minimum-path-length shape. A tree attaining the independent key-depth
profile bound is therefore fully optimal, without a large-baseline
assumption. For two-level keys, interlaced parity intervals recognize and
reconstruct such trees in $O(n)$ arithmetic operations and $O(n)$ auxiliary
space. This includes strictly alternating keys and gaps whenever $n+1$
is not a power of two, with arbitrary relative weight magnitudes, and
nonperiodic recognized patterns. Perfect sizes require separate checks.

A complementary representation groups runs of $(p_i,q_i+q_{i+1})$.
Block rewards telescope in skeleton rank, yielding an exact $O(nk)$-time,
$O(n)$-space shape optimizer for $k$ blocks. A mixed-node certificate
proves full optimality on a positive large-baseline family with arbitrary
block values. The parity method handles additional cases with $k=n$.
Routing preserves earlier fast paths; rejected inputs retain exact fallback.

A complementary affine-regret corridor retains only shape states whose
unavoidable reference cost can be offset by a signed residual gain bound.
For $n\ge32$, $p_i=B+i+u_i$, $q_i=B+i+v_i$, $B=4(n+1)^3$, integer errors
of total magnitude at most $n$ give $O(n\log^2 n)$ full construction;
independent unit errors give $O(n\log n)$. The optimum may change.

A published core-sparse Monge primitive also yields exact
$O(n^{3/2}\log^{5/2}(n+1))$ construction for nonnegative integer mixed weights
of total mass $O(n)$, and a centered extension gives
$O(n(1+\sqrt R)\log^3(n+1))$ arithmetic work for polynomial deviation $R$.
The implemented condensed backend loses to matched Knuth construction;
those negative measurements remain explicit. Positive ramp examples have
quadratic difference support yet compact exact formulas, and endpoint
counterexamples separate response size from composition sufficiency.
These are representation barriers, not general computational lower bounds.

Independent enumeration, cubic recurrences, source-hashed results, full
operation counts, and matched construction timings accompany the work.
Standard sliding maxima, Hirschberg traceback, established shape structure,
and prior OBST algorithms remain credited. The unrestricted subquadratic
question, mathematical priority, and broad practical superiority remain open.
\end{abstract}

\section{Model, scope, and prior work}
Keys have fixed inorder positions $0,\ldots,n-1$ and nonnegative weights
$p_0,\ldots,p_{n-1}$. The $n+1$ gaps have weights $q_0,\ldots,q_n$.
A key at depth $d$ contributes $p(d+1)$; a gap at depth $d$ contributes $qd$.
Charging an extra comparison at each gap adds the tree-independent constant
$\sum q_i$.

For a half-open key interval $[i,j)$, write
\[
 W(i,j)=\sum_{r=i}^{j-1}p_r+\sum_{r=i}^{j}q_r,\qquad C(i,i)=0.
\]
The exact recurrence is
\[
 C(i,j)=W(i,j)+\min_{i\le r<j}\{C(i,r)+C(r+1,j)\}.
\]
Knuth's monotone-root optimization computes the table in $O(n^2)$
time~\cite{knuth}. The central obstacle here is the number of states, not
merely the cost per state. We retain both kinds of weights throughout.

Larmore gives subquadratic exact construction under restrictions on long
runs of very low frequency items, together with an approximation
tradeoff~\cite{larmore}. Karpinski, Larmore, and Rytter combine exact small
intervals with a bounded top-level construction~\cite{klr}.
These results are essential context: an improvement on a restricted family
must not be described as the first subquadratic result.
This checkpoint does not make a comprehensive priority claim. The conditions
below were derived in this investigation; a targeted literature search did
not establish whether equivalent rules already exist.

All running-time bounds below count arithmetic operations and comparisons.
They are not bit-complexity bounds for unbounded integer weights.

\section{A sharper single-key contraction}
A key is a \emph{cherry} when its two children are external nodes. Replacing
a cherry with one gap is exact: if the key and its gaps have weights
$p,x,y$, their total is $s=p+x+y$. A cherry rooted at depth $d$ contributes
$s(d+1)$, whereas the replacement gap contributes $sd$.

\begin{theorem}[Single-key rule]
Let a key of weight $p$ have adjacent gap weights $x,y$.
If its immediate predecessor and successor keys have weights $a,b$ and
\[
 \min(a,b)\ \ge\ 2p+x+y,
\]
there is an optimal tree in which this key is a cherry.
At the left endpoint, it suffices that the successor weight is at least
$p+x$; at the right endpoint, it suffices that the predecessor weight is
at least $p+y$. Consequently contraction to a gap of weight $s=p+x+y$
satisfies $\OPT(\text{original})=\OPT(\text{contracted})+s$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Consider the key at depth $d$ in any tree. If its left subtree is nonempty,
the immediate predecessor has depth $d+l$, $l\ge1$. If its right subtree is
nonempty, the immediate successor has depth $d+r$, $r\ge1$.
Set the corresponding distance to zero for an empty internal side.

When $l\ge1$, move the predecessor to the chosen key's position, replacing
the predecessor's old position by its left child. Insert the chosen key as
a cherry at the leftmost gap of the former right subtree. Inorder order is
preserved. All other outcomes either retain their depths or become shallower.
The predecessor saves $la$, and the cost change is at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:left}
 -la+(r+1)p+(r-l+1)x+y.
\end{equation}
The mirrored successor exchange has change at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:right}
 -rb+(l+1)p+x+(l-r+1)y.
\end{equation}

If both sides are nonempty and $l\ge r$, substituting $a\ge2p+x+y$
in~\eqref{eq:left} gives
\[
 (r+1-2l)(p+x)+(1-l)y\le0.
\]
Use~\eqref{eq:right} when $r\ge l$. If only the left side is nonempty,
$r=0$ and $a\ge p+y$ makes~\eqref{eq:left} nonpositive.
The other case is symmetric. The stated interior condition implies these
one-sided conditions. At a global endpoint only the appropriate one-sided
case is possible. With both sides empty the key is already a cherry.

Applying the exchange to an optimum proves the existence claim.
Contraction subtracts exactly $s$. Conversely, every contracted tree
expands to a valid original tree at additional cost $s$, proving equality.
\end{proof}

The weaker condition $\min(a,b)\ge p+x+y$ is false.
For $p=(24,14,24)$ and $q=(1,0,9,0)$, the middle key has local mass 23
and neighbors of weight 24. Contracting it forces cost 142, while the
unrestricted optimum costs 130.

Maintain a doubly linked list of remaining keys and weighted gaps.
Initially queue every key; after a contraction, only its predecessor and
successor need rechecking. At most $3n$ queue entries are processed.
Saving the contracted tree at the new gap permits reconstruction.
Thus this pass takes $O(n)$ operations and space, and a Knuth solve on the
remaining $m$ keys gives total time $O(n+m^2)$. No confluence or
minimum-core-size property is claimed.

\section{Whole-block contraction}
Let $B=[i,j)$ be a nonempty proper interval, including its endpoint gaps
$q_i,q_j$. Put $W=W(i,j)$ and $C=C(i,j)$, with costs measured from a
stand-alone root of depth zero.

\begin{theorem}[Block rule]\label{thm:block}
If both boundary keys exist and their weights satisfy
\[
 p_{i-1}\ge W+C,\qquad p_j\ge W+C,
\]
there is an optimum in which all of $B$ forms one subtree.
For a prefix or suffix block, it suffices that the sole boundary key
has weight at least $C$. Contracting the block to one gap of weight $W$
then gives
\[
 \OPT(\text{original})=\OPT(\text{contracted})+C.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
In any tree, let $v$ be the lowest common ancestor of gaps $q_i,q_j$,
at depth $d$. Its key lies in $B$, and every outcome of $B$ descends
from $v$. Hence the original contribution of $B$ is at least $(d+1)W$.

Within the subtree rooted at $v$, delete the keys of $B$ from the inward
fringes of its two children. On the left, a deleted key has no retained
right child and can be suppressed in favor of its left child; on the
right the process is symmetric. Let the retained outside trees be $A,D$.
All their retained outcomes are at depths no greater than before.
If $A$ is nonempty, its maximum is boundary key $i-1$; if $D$ is
nonempty, its minimum is boundary key $j$.

Suppose both are nonempty, and their boundary keys had original depths
$d+l,d+r$. If $l\ge r$, remove the maximum key from $A$, replacing its
position by its left child, and make that key the new root at $v$.
Its left child is the remainder of $A$ and its right child is $D$.
Put an optimal tree for $B$ at the leftmost external position of $D$.
This position has depth at most $d+r+1$.
No outside outcome other than the promoted boundary moves deeper,
and that boundary saves $l p_{i-1}$.
The increase in the contribution of $B$ is at most
\[
 C+(d+r+1)W-(d+1)W=C+rW.
\]
Since $l\ge r\ge1$ and $p_{i-1}\ge W+C$, the saving is at least
$l(W+C)\ge rW+C$. The mirrored construction applies for $r\ge l$.

If only one retained outside tree is nonempty, promote its inward boundary
key to $v$ and attach the optimal block as the other child.
The block increase is at most $C$, while that boundary saves at least
its own weight. The interior hypothesis suffices, and for a global prefix
or suffix the weaker stated hypothesis suffices. If both outside trees
are empty, $B$ already occupies the subtree at $v$ and can be optimized
in place.

The construction never increases cost. Apply it to an optimum.
A block at depth $e$ contributes $C+eW$, while its replacement gap
contributes $eW$. Contraction and expansion now prove the equality.
\end{proof}

A balanced $k$-key tree has height
$h_k=\lceil\log_2(k+1)\rceil$ and cost at most $h_kW$.
Thus the interior test
\[
 \min(p_{i-1},p_j)\ge(1+h_k)W
\]
and endpoint test $p_{\rm boundary}\ge h_kW$ certify a block without first
solving it. These are sufficient conditions, not characterizations.

\section{An adaptive linear selection pass}
Build the maximum Cartesian tree of the key-weight sequence, breaking ties
deterministically. Its subtree intervals form a laminar family: intervals
are disjoint or nested. Compute their sizes and masses by one traversal and
prefix sums. Test each proper interval with the balanced-tree bound above.
This inspects only $O(n)$ candidates. The restriction to Cartesian intervals
is an algorithmic choice, not a claim that all useful blocks occur there.

For a threshold $b$, select the maximal certified intervals whose sizes
are at most $b$. Different selected intervals have a surviving separator
key between them. Contracting one therefore leaves every other selected
interval's weights and boundary-key weights unchanged, so the block theorem
applies successively. Write their sizes as $k_1,\ldots,k_s$, and
$m=n-\sum_t k_t$. The exact number of nonempty interval-DP states is
\[
 S(b)=\tri m+\sum_t\tri{k_t},\qquad \tri u=\frac{u(u+1)}2.
\]

We choose a threshold minimizing $S(b)$ without running these DPs.
For every certified interval $I$ of size $k$, find its maximal proper
certified descendants $J$. Immediately before $I$ becomes eligible,
these descendants are its selected blocks. Its activation therefore
changes the core size and block-state total by
\[
 \Delta m=-\left(k-\sum_J |J|\right),\qquad
 \Delta S_{\rm blocks}=\tri k-\sum_J\tri{|J|}.
\]
These sums are computed bottom-up. Bucket activation events by integer
size $1,\ldots,n$ and scan the buckets, maintaining $m$ and the block-state
total. There are $O(n)$ events and buckets. Equal-size intervals are disjoint;
evaluate a threshold after processing its entire bucket.
The empty selection $b=0$ is included.

\begin{corollary}
The selected threshold and blocks are found in $O(n)$ arithmetic operations
and space. Solving the blocks and surviving core by Knuth's method and
expanding their saved trees takes
\[
 O\!\left(n+m^2+\sum_t k_t^2\right)
\]
time and $O(n+m^2+\max_t k_t^2)$ space when block tables are released
after each solve. The chosen state count is no larger than $n(n+1)/2$.
Worst-case time remains $O(n^2)$.
\end{corollary}

The minimization is only over these size thresholds in this candidate
hierarchy, not over every possible partition. It does not minimize
wall-clock time. Preprocessing, table allocation, root scans, and
reconstruction still count as work.

For perspective, a certified partition with
$m=O(n^{2/3})$ surviving keys and every $k_t=O(n^{1/3})$ gives
$O(n^{4/3})$ time, since $\sum k_t^2\le(\max k_t)n$.
This is a conditional structural consequence, not an unrestricted bound.

\section{Recursive construction with a bounded preprocessing depth}
The implementation \texttt{recursive\_contract.py} combines the proved
components. At each call it applies the single-key pass, compares the
order-free lower bound of Section~\ref{sec:unordered} with a feasible
bisection-tree cost, and returns immediately on equality. Otherwise it
selects blocks as above. It recurses only if their combined table-state
estimate is at most three quarters of the full estimate; small components
and unsuccessful decompositions fall back to Knuth's method.

Suppose a component has $m>8$ keys after the single-key pass and is
successfully decomposed. Every child component of size $s$ satisfies
$s(s+1)\le\tfrac34m(m+1)$, hence $s<0.92m$.
Child sizes sum to $m$. Consequently the recursion has logarithmic depth,
and the total number of keys processed on any recursion level is at most
$n$. Linear selection and reconstruction cost $O(n\log n)$ in total.
The heap-based equality checks cost $O(n\log^2 n)$ over all levels.
If the terminal DP sizes are $r_t$, the construction therefore takes
\[
 O\!\left(n\log^2 n+\sum_t r_t^2\right)
\]
arithmetic operations. Its worst case is still quadratic. This threshold
prevents repeated expensive scans after arbitrarily small reductions.
Correctness follows by contraction, exact equality certificates, and induction
on the strictly smaller recursive components; reconstruction restores the
original key indices and gap subtrees.

The recursive implementation passed 3,000 further random inputs through
$n=30$ against the cubic oracle and direct tree evaluation. A separate nested
fixture checks composition across multiple decomposition levels.
Matched Python timings use five inputs per family, alternate which solver
runs first, and include preprocessing and reconstruction for both solvers.
At $n=1024$, the median runtime ratio (recursive divided by Knuth) was
0.134 on the positive-weight heavy-separator family; its median terminal
DP count was 30,017 versus 524,800. On random mixed weights the ratio was
0.986 and on near-uniform weights 1.001, which support no meaningful gain.
At $n=512$, the respective random and near-uniform ratios were 1.131 and
1.093: preprocessing can make the method slower. These are exploratory
measurements on one M4 host, not production-language or cross-machine claims.

\section{A certified shortcut through nearly complete shapes}
Let $I(T)$ be the sum of internal-node depths, with root depth zero.
The external path length is $I(T)+2n$, by induction on the two subtrees.
Consequently uniform key weight $a$ and gap weight $b$ give cost
\[
 (a+b)I(T)+(a+2b)n.
\]
For an arbitrary instance set $a=\min_i p_i$, $b=\min_j q_j$, and let
$H'$ be the order-free mixed-node Huffman bound of
Section~\ref{sec:unordered} on residual weights $p_i-a,q_j-b$.
These weights remain nonnegative. Write $I_*(n)$ for the minimum possible
unweighted internal path length, and let $C_*$ be the best weighted cost
among all trees with $I(T)=I_*(n)$.

\begin{samepage}
\begin{theorem}[Minimum-path-length certificate]
For $n\ge1$, if
\[
 C_*\ \le\ (a+b)(I_*(n)+1)+(a+2b)n+H',
\]
then a tree attaining $C_*$ is a globally optimal full OBST.
\end{theorem}
\end{samepage}
\begin{proof}
Split each outcome weight into its uniform baseline and residual. Every
tree outside the minimum-$I$ class has integer $I(T)\ge I_*(n)+1$, and its
residual cost is at least $H'$. Its full cost is therefore at least the
right-hand side. Within the class, $C_*$ is minimal by definition.
Equality is sufficient, including when $a+b=0$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Optimize the class without interval tables.}
Put $h=\lfloor\log_2(n+1)\rfloor$, $S=2^h$, $m=S-1$, and $r=n-m$.
Every minimum-$I$ tree has the complete upper skeleton with $m$ keys,
and expands exactly $r$ of its $S$ external slots into single-key cherries.
Indeed, a missing shallower slot could otherwise receive a deepest internal
leaf, strictly reducing $I$. Inorder relabeling preserves search-tree
feasibility in this unweighted shape argument. Thus
\[
 I_*(n)=(h-2)2^h+2+hr.
\]

Process skeleton slots $j=0,\ldots,S-1$ in inorder. State $(j,e)$ records
the number of processed slots and expansions, so the next input index is
$i=j+e$. An unexpanded slot charges $h q_i$; an expanded slot charges
$(h+1)(p_i+q_i+q_{i+1})$. Except after the last slot, also charge the next
skeleton key at comparison length
\[
 d_j=h-v_2(j+1),
\]
using $p_i$ after an unexpanded slot and $p_{i+1}$ after an expansion.
Here $v_2$ counts trailing zero bits. Exactly $r$ expansions are required,
so state $(j,e)$ is retained only when
\[
 \max(0,r-(S-j))\le e\le\min(r,j).
\]
With $s=\min(r,S-r)$, each stage has at most $s+1$ states and each state
has two outgoing choices. This computes $C_*$ and backpointers in
$O(n(1+s))$ time and space. A linear Cartesian-tree pass converts the
chosen comparison lengths into the labeled tree. No key or gap weights
are discarded in these transitions.

There is an exact early rejection test. Let $F$ be the cost obtained by
assigning independently sorted key and gap weights to the shallowest
depths of a minimum-$I$ shape. This is feasible for the order-free residual
relaxation, so $H'\le F-[(a+b)I_*+(a+2b)n]$. Therefore $C_*>F+a+b$
proves the certificate cannot succeed. Otherwise the sorted lists are
reused in a two-queue Huffman implementation: nonnegative weights and
nondecreasing internal weights make the sequence of merged weights
nondecreasing. This adds linear work after sorting. The queue optimization
is standard and is not claimed as novel.

The implementation attempts this DP only when
$s\le\lfloor\log_2 n\rfloor+1$. Including the residual bound,
preprocessing, and reconstruction, a successful attempt costs
$O(n\log n)$ arithmetic operations and space. A skipped or failed attempt
falls back to Knuth, or to the preceding recursive construction. Both
versions remain exact and have quadratic worst-case time. The test is a
certificate, not an assumption that near-uniform inputs have balanced optima.
Novelty of this shortcut has not been established.

\paragraph{Verification and limits.}
Minimum-$I$ optimization matched independent enumeration of all tree shapes
through eight keys on 360 weighted inputs. Both fallback versions matched
independent cubic DP and direct tree costs on 3,000 further mixed-weight
inputs through thirty keys; the unrestricted certificate succeeded on 768.
At $n=1,024$, one seeded input with every weight in $\{999,1000,1001\}$
used 2,048 shape states, 3,070 transitions, 10,511 sorting comparisons,
and 1,350 merge comparisons, certifying cost 19,479,590. The nonminimum-$I$ lower bound was
19,480,123. Knuth used 524,800 states and 1,050,139 root candidates.
These work units have different costs; the state ratio is not a timing claim.

The selected perturbation sweep certified amplitude 8 around 1,000 at
$n=256$, amplitude 4 at $n=512$, and amplitude 1 at $n=1,024$; larger tested
amplitudes at those sizes failed. These are seeded observations, not
universal thresholds. Ordinary random inputs failed the certificate.
Sizes 384, 768, and 1,000 deliberately skip the shortcut because their
minimum-$I$ shape space exceeds the logarithmic width budget.

Six matched trials per fixture used all six orderings of Knuth, recursive
contraction, and the combined solver, with identical inputs and correctness
checks outside timing. On Apple M4 with Python 3.9.6, median per-trial
combined/Knuth time ratios at $n=1,024$ were 0.03934 for near-uniform,
0.10478 for heavy separators, 1.05454 for random, 0.91929 for log-uniform,
and 1.03117 for gap spikes. All six near-uniform cases certified. The skipped
near-uniform controls at $n=768$ and $n=1,000$ gave ratios 1.17886 and 1.04896.
At $n=512$ the combined method was about 21\% slower than recursive
contraction alone on heavy separators. These exploratory timings include
sorting, lower bounds, and reconstruction; they do not establish broad
superiority. The final early-rejection and two-queue implementations were
also checked against the original heap certificate on all 3,000 inputs.

\paragraph{Optimize the baseline rather than fix it.}
The largest subtractable baseline need not give the strongest certificate.
Set $a_0=\min p_i$, $b_0=\min q_j$, and use
$a=a_0-\delta$, $b=b_0-\delta$ for an integer
$0\le\delta\le\lfloor(a_0+b_0)/2\rfloor$.
The residual weights remain nonnegative and $a+b\ge0$. Although one
baseline term may become negative, it is only an algebraic decomposition;
the input probabilities are unchanged. Let $H_\delta$ denote the residual
order-free optimum. The exclusion bound is
\[
 B(\delta)=(a_0+b_0-2\delta)(I_*+1)
              +(a_0+2b_0-3\delta)n+H_\delta.
\]
Every tree outside the minimum-$I$ class costs at least $B(\delta)$.
Thus any sampled price with $C_*\le B(\delta)$ certifies the same candidate.

For a fixed residual tree, increasing $\delta$ adds one unit to every
key and gap weight, changing its cost at rate $2I+3n$. Consequently
$B$ is a pointwise minimum of affine functions, hence concave. A minimizing
residual tree supplies a supergradient
\[
 g=2(I-I_*-1),\qquad
 B(x)\le B(\delta)+g(x-\delta).
\]
If $g>0$, smaller prices cannot improve the current value; if $g<0$,
larger prices cannot improve it. If $g=0$, the value is globally maximal.
Binary interval elimination, retaining the best sample, therefore finds
the maximum over allowed integer prices when run to completion.

The same linear oracle supplies $I$. Give every original outcome secondary
weight1, order queue items lexicographically by residual mass and number
of original outcomes, and sum both merge coordinates. The first gives
$H_\delta$ and the second gives $2I+3n$. This is the Huffman exchange
argument with an infinitesimal positive common perturbation, choosing
minimum $I$ among residual-cost ties. The weight order is independent of
$\delta$, so no additional sorting is needed. Each price costs $O(n)$.

The fixed-profile ceiling extends to all prices:
$B(\delta)\le F+a_0+b_0-2\delta$. Therefore an input rejected by the
existing profile test cannot be rescued by this search. The implementation
tries prices only after the old certificate fails while passing that test,
and permits at most $\lfloor\log_2n\rfloor+1$ extra oracle calls.
This preserves the $O(n\log n)$ attempted-shortcut bound. Reaching the cap
does not establish that the best price was found; every sampled certificate
is still valid, and failure invokes the exact fallback.

The lexicographic oracle matched 400 exhaustive shape-profile comparisons
through eight keys. Uncapped search matched exhaustive evaluation of all
allowed integer prices on 1,200 inputs. On 3,000 additional full-OBST
inputs through thirty keys, both capped fallback versions matched cubic
DP and direct tree costs; 87 previously unsuccessful unrestricted
certificates were recovered. A separate seeded sweep recovered the
$n=1,024$, amplitude 2 input with two additional oracle calls, and the
$n=2,048$, amplitude 1 input with three. Larger tested perturbations
usually exceeded the profile ceiling. This applies standard concave-price
optimization to the certificate; it is not a general subquadratic result.

In the seeded 2,048-key case, the candidate costs 43,032,446; the bound
rises from 43,031,347 at $\delta=0$ to 43,032,459 at $\delta=125$.
The three extra passes use 6,144 contractions and 6,141 pair comparisons,
in addition to 4,096 shape states, 6,142 transitions, 20,784 sorting
comparisons, and 2,752 original merge comparisons. Knuth uses 2,098,176
interval states and 4,198,710 root candidates on that input.

A separate six-trial comparison uses all orderings of Knuth, the old
hybrid, and the price-search hybrid. All six $n=1,024$, amplitude 2 cases
and all six $n=2,048$, amplitude 1 cases were newly certified. Median
per-trial price-search/Knuth ratios were respectively 0.04953 and 0.06128.
At $n=2,048$, amplitude 2 still failed, with ratio 0.99391. Ordinary random
and gap-spike controls at $n=1,024$ gave ratios 1.06492 and 1.12941.
The already-certified $n=1,024$, amplitude 1 control made no extra oracle
calls yet showed a price-search/old-hybrid ratio of 1.13564. This variability
limits interpretation of small timing differences; operation counts and
all raw observations are retained. The host was Apple M4 with Python 3.9.6.


\section{Exact bounded-excess construction by dyadic intervals}
The candidate class can be expanded beyond minimum internal path length.
This is necessary even on some mildly nonuniform inputs. For $n=255$, set
all gap weights to $16$ and all key weights to $16$, except keys $8j+1$
which have weight $17$. The unique minimum-$I$ tree costs $61{,}680$;
Knuth gives optimum $61{,}656$, attained
with $I=I_*+2$. The algorithm below constructs and certifies this optimum.
It retains both kinds of weight and never assumes that the restricted
optimum is globally optimal.

\paragraph{An exact representation.}
An ordered full binary tree is a tiling of $[0,1]$ by consecutive aligned
dyadic intervals: a gap at depth $d$ has width $2^{-d}$. A leaf starting
at $x$ must satisfy $x/2^{-d}\in\mathbb Z$. Conversely such a tiling is a
complete prefix code and determines an ordered full tree. Alignment, not
just the Kraft equality, enforces order. On a grid of height $H$, an
interior boundary at integer coordinate $Y$ corresponds to a key with
comparison length $H-\nu_2(Y)$. Thus gap and key costs are both local in
a left-to-right tiling construction.

\begin{theorem}[Bounded-excess construction]
For $n\ge1$, let $N=n+1$, $h=\lfloor\log_2N\rfloor$, $S=2^h$, $r=N-S$, and
$s=\min(r,S-r)$. For any nonnegative integer budget $k$, the minimum
full weighted cost among all trees with $I\le I_*+k$ can be found and
reconstructed in
\[
 O\bigl(n(s+k+1)(k+1)^2 2^k\bigr)
\]
arithmetic operations, using
$O\bigl(n(s+k+1)(k+1)2^k\bigr)$ space for backpointers.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Measure dyadic widths in units of $2^{-h}$ and put $w=2^{h-d}$.
For integral depth $d$, the potential
\[
 f(d)=d-h-2+2^{h+1-d}=d-h-2+2w
\]
is nonnegative, with zeros exactly at $h$ and $h+1$. The minimum external
path length is $hN+2r$. Since external path length equals $I+2n$ and
$\sum_i w_i=S$, every full tree satisfies
\[
 \sum_i f(d_i)=\sum_i d_i-(h+2)N+2S=I-I_*.
\]
Every depth in the budgeted class therefore has $f(d)\le k$. This implies
$d\le H=h+k+2$, and only $O(k+1)$ integral depths are possible.
For integral $d$, direct substitution gives
\[
 \tfrac12-\tfrac12 f(d)\le w\le1+f(d).
\]
Apply these inequalities to a prefix of $i$ leaves and to its remaining
suffix, each having potential at most $k$. The cumulative position $y$
must obey
\[
 \max\left(\tfrac{i-k}{2},i-r-k\right)
 \le y\le
 \min\left(i+k,\tfrac{S-r+i+k}{2}\right).
\]
This interval has width at most both $r+2k$ and $(S-r)/2+k$, hence
$O(s+k)$.

Set $K=2^{k+2}$. A state after $i$ leaves stores integral drift
$u=K(y-i)$ and spent potential $z=K\sum f(d)$. For depth $d$, let
$W=2^{H-d}$. Require $(iK+u)\bmod W=0$ and update
\[
 u'=u+W-K,\qquad z'=z+K(d-h)+2(W-K).
\]
Retain the position band and $0\le z'\le Kk$. Charge $q_i d$ and, for
$i>0$, charge $p_{i-1}(H-\nu_2(iK+u))$. Start at $(u,z)=(0,0)$ and
finish after $N$ leaves with $u=-rK$. Every retained complete path is an
aligned full tiling with excess at most $k$; every tree in the class
passes the same tests. The DP therefore optimizes exactly this class.
For fixed $u$, the congruence $z\equiv2u\pmod K$ leaves at most $k+1$
possible spent potentials. There are $O((s+k+1)2^k)$ positions per stage
and $O(k+1)$ choices per state, proving the bounds. The bands admit
direct array indexing; the Python prototype uses sparse dictionaries. Backpointers recover
gap depths; the boundary lengths reconstruct the tree in linear time.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Perfect-size refinement.}
When $N=S$, use instead
$f_0(d)=d-h+\tfrac32(2^{h-d}-1)$. It is nonnegative on integer depths,
vanishes only at $h$, and also sums to $I-I_*$. The inequality
$|2^{h-d}-1|\le2f_0(d)$ and zero total drift imply $|y-i|\le k$.
Here $H=h+k+1$, $K=2^{k+1}$, and $z=2K\sum f_0$. Its increment is
$2K(d-h)+3(W-K)$, and $z\equiv3u\pmod{2K}$. The implementation uses this
smaller grid at perfect sizes, with the same asymptotic bound at $s=0$.

\paragraph{Certify against all other trees.}
Let $C_k$ be the computed candidate, $a=\min p_i$, $b=\min q_i$, and
$H'$ the order-free bound on residual weights. Every excluded tree has
$I\ge I_*+k+1$, so it costs at least
\[
 B_k=(a+b)(I_*+k+1)+(a+2b)n+H'.
\]
Thus $C_k\le B_k$ proves global optimality. Sorting and the two-queue
bound cost $O(n\log n)$ and are included in the counters. The constructor
uses $k=2$ and attempts the shortcut only when
$s\le\operatorname{bit\_length}(n)$. Its attempted work is $O(n\log n)$;
a failed certificate or skipped size falls back to Knuth. The total worst
case remains quadratic. Arbitrary growth of $k$ is exponential, and wide
shape spaces remain quadratic even for fixed $k$.

\section{A split-height construction covering every input size}
The preceding shape band can be wide even at zero excess. We therefore
implement the split-height/Monge mechanism already sketched by Karpinski,
Larmore, and Rytter in Section~5 of their longer preprint~\cite{klrheight}.
That section explicitly supplies the fixed-height Monge property and the
two-part construction. It is different from Section~5 of the six-page
conference version~\cite{klr}. No priority claim is made for this mechanism.
The present experiment makes the tiling, matrix padding, reconstruction,
and separate unrestricted certificate explicit.

\paragraph{Two scales of dyadic intervals.}
Fix an external height limit $H=L+U$ and write $K=2^L$, $B=2^U$.
Precompute all relative costs $C_h(i,j)$ for $0\le h\le L$ and
$0\le j-i\le2^h-1$, using
\[
 C_h(i,j)=W(i,j)+\min_r\{C_{h-1}(i,r)+C_{h-1}(r+1,j)\}.
\]
Empty intervals have zero cost; the root range enforces both child height
capacities. Monge inequalities at fixed height give monotone minimizing
roots as the endpoints move. Choose leftmost ties and bound each root by
the adjacent shorter intervals. Search ranges telescope to $O(n)$ for
each interval length. Summing over lengths and heights costs $O(n2^L)$.

Divide the unit dyadic interval into $B$ top-grid blocks. Every tree of
height at most $H$ consists of lower subtrees contained in individual
blocks, together with shallower gaps occupying aligned groups of blocks.
Let $D_b(i)$ be the minimum partial cost after consuming $i$ gaps and
reaching grid boundary $b$. Keys through $i-2$ have already been charged.
For $b>0$, put
\[
 R_b(i)=D_b(i)+p_{i-1}(U-v_2(b));\qquad R_0(0)=0.
\]
The boundary key has exactly this comparison length. There are two types
of transition:
\begin{align*}
 D_{b+1}(j)&\gets\min\{D_{b+1}(j),
 R_b(i)+C_L(i,j-1)+U W(i,j-1)\}, && i<j\le i+K,\\
 D_{b+w}(i+1)&\gets\min\{D_{b+w}(i+1),
 R_b(i)+q_i(U-\log_2 w)\}. &&
\end{align*}
The second transition requires a power of two $w\ge2$, $w\mid b$, and
$b+w\le B$. Width-one gaps are zero-key lower subtrees in the first
transition. Start at $D_0(0)=0$ and finish at $D_B(n+1)$. Dyadic alignment
proves that these paths represent every height-feasible tree and only
such trees. Backpointers recover every lower tree and every boundary key.

\paragraph{Linear matrix searches with feasible padding.}
At fixed $b$, the first transition is a banded Monge matrix in $i,j$;
row additions and the separable interval mass preserve the inequality.
Reachable counts $i$ are contiguous: the coarsest tiling of the prefix
has $\operatorname{popcount}(b)$ leaves, and successive refinement gives
every count through $bK$, truncated to $n$ before another transition.
Search output columns from $i_{\min}+1$ through
$\min(n+1,i_{\max}+K)$, each of which has a feasible input row.

SMAWK requires care outside the band. Let
$S=(H+1)(\sum p+\sum q)+1$, larger than every feasible prefix cost.
In the transposed matrix with row $j$ and candidate column $i$, use
\[
 A(j,i)=\begin{cases}
 S+j-K-i,&i<j-K,\\
 R_b(i)+C_L(i,j-1)+UW(i,j-1),&j-K\le i<j,\\
 S+i-j+1,&i\ge j.
 \end{cases}
\]
This padding preserves total monotonicity. For $i_1<i_2$, once $i_2$
is preferred at a row, it remains preferred as $j$ increases: within the
feasible band this is Monge; outside it, distances decrease toward the
band and increase away from it, and finite entries beat all padding.
When the candidates straddle opposite invalid sides, their cost difference
is $i_1+i_2+K+1-2j$, also decreasing. Ties preserve the same non-strict
implication. Thus SMAWK finds leftmost minima in $O(n)$ queries per grid
boundary. Padding by tied infinities does not supply this argument.

Across all boundaries there are $B-1$ aligned choices of coarse width,
so the second transitions and scans also cost $O(nB)$. Choosing
$L=\min(H,\lfloor H/2\rfloor+1)$ gives
\[
 O\bigl(n(2^L+2^U)\bigr)=O(n2^{H/2})
\]
work and storage including backpointers. Consequently a fixed extra height
$H=\lceil\log_2(n+1)\rceil+c$ gives $O(n^{3/2})$ attempted work at
\emph{every} size. The prototype uses sparse dictionaries; the bounded
integer state coordinates also admit direct array indexing.

\begin{lemma}[Minimum path length with a tall path]
For $1\le R\le n$, the minimum internal path length $J(n,R)$ among
$n$-key trees of external height at least $R$ is obtained by forcing a
path of $R$ internal nodes and filling the remaining available positions
in increasing order of depth. The excluded class is empty if $R>n$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Every tree in the class contains such a path, contributing $R(R-1)/2$.
The remaining nodes form an ancestor-closed subset of its free forest:
one side root at each depth $1,\ldots,R-1$, and two roots at depth $R$.
The $n-R$ shallowest positions in this forest are ancestor-closed and
minimize the depth sum among all subsets of that size. Their addition
constructs a feasible tree attaining the bound. Directions of the forced
path do not change the forest's depth counts.
\end{proof}
A level-count implementation computes $J$ in $O(R+\log(n+1))$ work.

\begin{theorem}[Certificate against taller trees]
Let $C_H$ be the exact height-restricted optimum above, and let $Q$ be
the order-free mixed-node Huffman lower bound for residual weights
$p-a,q-b$, where $a=\min p$, $b=\min q$. If $H<n$ and
\[
 C_H\le(a+b)J(n,H+1)+(a+2b)n+Q,
\]
the constructed tree is globally optimal. If $H\ge n$, there is no
height restriction and certification is automatic.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Every excluded tree has internal path length at least $J(n,H+1)$.
Its baseline contribution is $(a+b)I+(a+2b)n$ and its residual cost
is at least $Q$. Within the height class, $C_H$ is optimal.
\end{proof}
Sorting and the residual oracle add $O(n\log n)$ work. Failed tests invoke
Knuth, so the full constructor remains quadratic in the worst case.
The certificate never assumes success from near-uniform weights.

\section{A variable interval frontier for exact full construction}
The height test can fail even when its candidate is optimal. We now use a
class guaranteed in advance to contain an unrestricted optimum. The
short-interval/top-skeleton architecture is established work of Larmore
and Karpinski--Larmore--Rytter~\cite{larmore,klr}; the experiment compares
a uniform width envelope with a variable interval contour. Both use the
same safe mass guarantee and matrix infrastructure. This is not a priority
claim for the architecture or its restricted subquadratic regime.

\paragraph{A failed transfer of the sharper mass constant.}
The initial implementation used $2W/F_{d+3}$ as the maximum internal
subtree mass at edge depth $d$, following a literal transfer of the
Fibonacci formula in~\cite{hlm}. Its first full-input check failed: the
resulting class could be empty. The positive two-key example
\[
 p=(1,1),\qquad q=(1,100,1)
\]
explains the failure. Both trees cost 206; the total mass is 104 and the
internal child has mass 102. Thus $102>2\cdot104/3$ at depth one. Charging
an additional comparison at every gap is a tree-independent constant and
does not remove this violation. We preserve the counterexample and use
the following independently justified, weaker constant in our model.
This does not refute the KLR architecture or its asymptotic guarantees.

\begin{lemma}[A sufficient mixed-weight subtree bound]\label{lem:mass}
If $S$ is an internal subtree at edge depth $d$ in an optimal full OBST
of total outcome mass $W$, then
\[
 W(S)\le W/F_{d+1},\qquad F_1=F_2=1.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $X$ be an internal grandchild of $v$, with parent $y$, and let $O$
be the mass inside $v$'s subtree but outside $y$'s, including key $v$.
For a straight two-edge path, rotating $y$ upward changes cost by
$O-p_y-W(X)$. For a bent path, promoting $X$ with a double rotation
changes cost by $O-p_X-W(X)$. Optimality therefore implies $O\ge W(X)$
in either case. These formulas include all external gap weights.

Going upward from $S$, write $T_0=S,T_1,\ldots,T_d=T$. Nonnegativity
gives $W(T_1)\ge W(T_0)$, and the grandchild inequality gives
$W(T_i)\ge W(T_{i-1})+W(T_{i-2})$ for $i\ge2$.
Induction gives $W(T_d)\ge F_{d+1}W(S)$. Weak inequalities include
zero weights and ties.
\end{proof}
This lemma concerns internal subtrees. A heavy interior external gap can
have nearly all the mass at depth two; zero-key subtrees remain permitted.
The Fibonacci approach is prior work~\cite{hlm}; sharpness of this
particular sufficient bound is not claimed.

\paragraph{Store a contour instead of a global width.}
For fixed $d$, let $E_d(i)$ be the largest $j>i$ satisfying
$F_{d+1}W(i,j)\le W$, or $i$ if there is none. Empty intervals always
have stored cost zero. Nonnegative weights imply that $E_d(i)$ is
nondecreasing and the domain is closed under taking subintervals.
A two-pointer scan computes it in $O(n)$ work. Write
\[
 S_d=\sum_{i=0}^n(E_d(i)-i).
\]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:mass}, every internal subtree at depth $d$ of an
optimal tree belongs to the domain. For comparison, the uniform version
sets $\ell_d=\max_i(E_d(i)-i)$ and stores
\[
 U_d=\ell_d(n+1)-\ell_d(\ell_d+1)/2\ \ge S_d
\]
intervals. Both classes contain an unrestricted optimum.

\begin{lemma}[Root-search work on a hereditary contour]
Computing all unrestricted interval optima in this domain takes
$O(n+S_d)$ work, with at most $3S_d$ tested roots.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Bucket only existing cells by length in $O(n+S_d)$ work. Knuth's
recurrence applies because every child and adjacent shorter interval is
present. Let $r(i,j)$ be the leftmost optimal root and put
$t(i,j)=r(i,j)-i\ge0$. For length at least two, the root count is
$2+t(i+1,j)-t(i,j-1)$. Sum over the domain. A positive occurrence
$t(a,b)$ cancels its negative occurrence whenever $(a,b+1)$ is present.
An unmatched positive occurrence requires $b=E_d(a)$ and
$E_d(a-1)=E_d(a)$. There is at most one per row, of size at most
$E_d(a)-a$. Discard unmatched negative occurrences. Adding the length-one
cells proves the $3S_d$ bound. Scanning nonexistent diagonal cells would
lose this saving; the buckets are essential.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Compose the exact interval costs.}
Use $2^d$ top-grid blocks and the preceding dyadic transitions, replacing
height-limited lower costs by these unrestricted interval costs. A block
starting after $i$ gaps can finish after $j$ exactly when
$i<j\le E_d(i)+1$. It contributes $C(i,j-1)+dW(i,j-1)$ and the
starting boundary key cost $p_{i-1}(d-v_2(b))$ at $b>0$.
Coarse gaps retain their aligned power-of-two skip transitions. Every
optimal tree is represented, while every reconstructed candidate is a
valid full tree. Thus the resulting optimum is unrestricted.

The variable band needs another implementation detail. Reachable input
rows, and their union of feasible output columns, may have holes. Scan
once per top boundary to retain exactly output columns covered by some
reachable row. Put $L(j)=\min\{i:E_d(i)+1\ge j\}$, a nondecreasing
sequence with $L(j)\le j-1$. In the transposed matrix, use finite true
entries when $L(j)\le i<j$, and padding
\[
 Z+L(j)-i\quad(i<L(j)),\qquad
 Z+i-j+1\quad(i\ge j),\qquad Z=(n+d+1)W+1.
\]
All finite prefix costs are smaller than $Z$. Within the band, exact
interval costs are Monge. Across padding, opposite-side differences
\mbox{$i_1+i_2+1-j-L(j)$} are nonincreasing; same-side preferences also
move right. Thus the matrix is totally monotone,
even after removing arbitrary rows and columns, and every searched column
has a feasible minimum. SMAWK, scans, and coarse transitions together
cost $O(n2^d)$. Backpointers recover every key and gap contribution.

\begin{theorem}[Exact mass-frontier constructor]
The adaptive implementation constructs a full optimal BST in
\[
 O\!\left(n\log(n+1)+\min_{d\ge0}\{S_d+n2^d\}\right)
\]
arithmetic work. The uniform-envelope implementation replaces $S_d$ by
$U_d$. At the selected depth, storage is $O(n+S_d+n2^d)$ or
$O(n+U_d+n2^d)$ respectively. Both remain quadratic in the worst case.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The interval and composition arguments establish exactness at every depth.
Start with depth zero and the full Knuth table. Evaluate successive depths
while $n2^d$ is smaller than the best work proxy so far; each contour costs
$O(n)$. No later depth can improve the proxy because its top term alone
is too large. At most $O(\log(n+1))$ depths are evaluated. Depth zero
uses Knuth directly, omitting a redundant top pass and its lower-order
$n$ proxy term. The preceding work and storage bounds now apply.
\end{proof}
This constructor has no posterior certificate failure followed by a second
solve. It may select the full table initially. The Python prototype uses
dictionaries; bounded integer top coordinates also allow direct arrays.
When each key has weight at least $a>0$ and $W=O(na)$, we have
$S_d=O(n^2/\phi^d)$, where $\phi=(1+\sqrt5)/2$. Balancing against
$n2^d$ recovers the known exponent $1+\log_{2\phi}2\simeq1.59023$.

\paragraph{A scoped asymptotic separation.}
Let $n$ range over fourth powers and $L=n^{3/4}$. Give every key and gap
weight $A=n^2$, except a central block of $L$ keys and its $L+1$ gaps,
all of weight one. Every weight is positive and $W=\Theta(nA)$.
An admitted interval contains at most $m=O(n/F_{d+1})$ heavy keys.
Intervals wholly inside the light block number $O(L^2)$. Other starts
contribute $O(nm)$ ordinary endpoints; only $O(m)$ outside starts can
reach across a block boundary and gain $O(L)$ endpoints, and starts
inside add at most $O(Lm)$ heavy endpoints. Consequently
\[
 S_d=O(n^2/F_{d+1}+L^2).
\]
With $d=\lfloor\log_{2\phi}n\rfloor$, the adaptive work is
$O(n^{1+\log_{2\phi}2}+n^{3/2})=O(n^{1.59024})$.

Every uniform-envelope plan, however, has proxy $\Omega(nL)$.
If $2^d\ge L$, the top term suffices. Otherwise
$F_{d+1}=O(L^{\log_2\phi})$, so $W/F_{d+1}$ exceeds the entire light
block's $O(L)$ mass. Hence $\ell_d\ge L$ and the uniform table has
at least $nL/2$ cells. This gives $\Omega(n^{1.75})$ at every depth.
The separation is within the specified architecture, not against every
prior OBST algorithm or combinations of our shortcuts. In particular,
this light block also offers an opportunity for certified contraction.

Both modes were checked against Knuth on this positive family at
$n=16,81,256,625$, with cubic checks also at the first two sizes. Larger
instances were used only for plan counting. At $n=65{,}536$, the chosen
adaptive and uniform proxies were 119,041,540 and 361,608,522, both at
depth nine. These are $S_d+n2^d$ and $U_d+n2^d$, not measured full-solve
operation totals or timings. All parameter counts are retained.

\section{Dominant-gap separators and local mass frontiers}
The global mass bound can admit many unnecessary intervals when a single
heavy outcome dominates otherwise ordinary regions. We first split at
certified outcomes, then apply the frontier constructor with each terminal
component's own total mass. The following exchanges retain all key and gap
weights. Their priority is not established.

\begin{lemma}[Restriction to an ordered interval]\label{lem:restriction}
A BST restricted to a contiguous key interval and its endpoint gaps has
no greater depth for any retained outcome. If the old root is outside the
retained key interval, every retained outcome loses at least one comparison.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Delete each comparison at an outside key and follow the branch containing
all retained outcomes. Comparisons at retained keys remain. This gives the
required BST, with each retained search path a subsequence of its old path.
The argument also applies to an empty key interval containing one gap.
\end{proof}

Fix an interior gap $g$ in a current component. Its adjacent keys are
$a=g-1$ and $b=g$. Let $L$ contain the keys strictly left of $a$, with
its endpoint gaps, and let $R$ contain those strictly right of $b$ with
its endpoint gaps. Their masses are $W_L,W_R$, and the component mass is
\[
 W=W_L+p_a+q_g+p_b+W_R.
\]
\begin{theorem}[Interior-gap separator]\label{thm:gap-separator}
If
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gap-separator}
 q_g+\min(p_a,p_b)\ \ge\ W_L+W_R,
\end{equation}
there exists an optimal tree with gap $g$ at depth two. Its top two keys
are $a,b$, in either orientation, and its remaining subtrees are $L,R$.
Writing $C_L,C_R$ for their unrestricted optimal relative costs gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gap-cost}
 \OPT=C_L+C_R+W+q_g+\min(p_a+W_L,\ p_b+W_R).
\end{equation}
At equality, other optimal trees need not have this shape.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Take any tree in which $g$ has depth at least three. Restrict it to $L,R$
using Lemma~\ref{lem:restriction}. If its root lies in $L$, make $a$ the
new root, attach restricted $L$ on its left, and make $b$ its right child,
with $g$ on $b$'s left and restricted $R$ on its right. Every $L$ outcome
deepens by at most one. Every $R$ outcome lost the old root comparison,
then gains two, so also deepens by at most one. Key $a$ saves at least one
comparison, key $b$ does not deepen, and gap $g$ saves at least one.
The cost change is at most $W_L+W_R-p_a-q_g$. For an old root in $R$,
the mirror exchange costs at most $W_L+W_R-p_b-q_g$.

If the old root is $a$, keep it and promote $b$ to its right child.
The $L$ depths do not increase and $R$ depths increase by at most one.
As $a,b$ are adjacent and $g$ was deeper than two, $b$ had depth at least
two and saves at least one comparison. The change is at most
$W_R-p_b-q_g$. The case with old root $b$ is symmetric. Condition
\eqref{eq:gap-separator} makes each bound nonpositive, so the exchange can
be applied to an optimum. An interior gap cannot have depth below two;
at depth two its bounding ancestor keys must be exactly $a,b$.

Both orientations leave the same two independent optimization problems.
With root $a$, the cost is $C_L+C_R+W+q_g+p_b+W_R$; with root $b$ it is
$C_L+C_R+W+q_g+p_a+W_L$. Minimize these two values after solving each
outside interval once. This proves~\eqref{eq:gap-cost} without duplicated
recursive branches.
\end{proof}

Replacing the smaller neighboring weight in~\eqref{eq:gap-separator} by
the larger one is false. For $p=(100,1,50)$ and $q=(1,1,1,1)$, the
weakened test accepts $g=1$ since $101\ge53$. Yet the full optimum is
212 and forcing this gap to depth two costs 261. The valid test rejects
it since $2<53$. This strictly positive counterexample is retained.

\paragraph{Endpoint and key certificates.}
For a component's first key and left endpoint gap, $2(p_i+q_i)\ge W$
suffices to put that key at the root in some optimum; the right endpoint
is symmetric. If its original depth is $h\ge1$, its endpoint gap has
depth $h+1$. Promotion saves $h(p_i+q_i)$ while restricting and reattaching
the other interval increases cost by at most $W-p_i-q_i$. Likewise,
$2p_r\ge W$ certifies an arbitrary key $r$ as root: promote it and attach
the two restrictions, increasing every other depth by at most one while
saving at least one comparison on $r$. These are sufficient conditions,
not a general largest-weight root rule.

\paragraph{Avoiding quadratic separator scans.}
Components remain contiguous intervals of the original input, with no
weight aggregation. Store the static scores
\[
 G_j=2q_j+p_{j-1}+p_j+\min(p_{j-1},p_j),\qquad K_j=2p_j.
\]
An interior gap in component $[i,k)$ satisfies~\eqref{eq:gap-separator}
exactly when $G_j\ge W(i,k)$, for $i<j<k$. Two range-maximum segment
trees find qualifying gaps and keys in $O(\log(n+1))$ work per query,
after $O(n)$ construction. Prefix sums give component masses in constant
work. Each separator removes one or two keys, and terminal intervals are
disjoint. Only terminal slices are copied, so a long chain does not cause
quadratic copying or rescanning. The implementation tests endpoints first
and builds the indexes only when an endpoint test fails on both sides.
All-zero components are returned as balanced trees directly.

\begin{theorem}[Local-frontier composition]\label{thm:local-frontier}
For terminal components $t$ with $m_t$ keys and local admissible-interval
counts $S_{t,d}$, the constructor is exact and uses
\[
 O\!\left(n\log(n+1)+\sum_t\min_d\{S_{t,d}+m_t2^d\}\right)
\]
arithmetic work. If $m_t\le b$ for every terminal, the bound is
$O(n\log(n+1)+nb)$. Sequential reuse of terminal scratch storage gives
$O(n+\max_t\{S_{t,d_t}+m_t2^{d_t}\})$ space at selected depths $d_t$.
The unrestricted worst case remains quadratic.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The separator identities reduce the optimum to disjoint exact subproblems.
There are $O(n)$ separator calls and $\sum_t m_t\le n$. The local frontier
bounds apply separately, including parameter selection since
$\sum_t m_t\log(m_t+1)\le n\log(n+1)$. Each terminal may select depth
zero and use its full Knuth table, so $\sum_t m_t^2\le nb$ when all sizes
are at most $b$. Include the reconstructed output and separator stack in
$O(n)$ space; completed terminal scratch arrays need not remain allocated.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Two exact structured regimes.}
For $p_i=q_i=2^i$ and $q_n=2^n$, the total mass is $3\cdot2^n-2$ and
the rightmost key/gap pair has mass $3\cdot2^{n-1}$. The right endpoint
certificate passes, and the remaining prefix has the same form. The
optimal chain is constructed with $n$ endpoint decisions, no range index,
and no terminal DP: $O(n)$ arithmetic work. At the final single key the
implementation selects the equivalent left endpoint rule. Weights have
$O(n)$ bits, so this is not a linear bit-complexity assertion.

For a different positive mixed family, recursively plant a heavy central
gap separating two outside intervals, stopping below 32 keys. Set each
planted weight to four times its component mass after its children have
been planted. Each planted gap satisfies the theorem, and all terminal
sizes are below 32. This gives $O(n\log n)$ arithmetic work on the family,
including varying positive background weights on both keys and gaps.
Neither regime is an unrestricted subquadratic result.

\paragraph{A positive obstruction to the combined constructor.}
For $n\ge5$, set every key and gap weight to one except
$q_1=q_{n-1}=M=n^3$. Then $W=2M+2n-1$. The largest gap score is
$2M+3<W$, endpoint scores are four, and key scores are two, so no separator
fires. Indeed the frontier planner selects depth zero and the full table.
To see this, write $\tau(k)=k(k+1)/2$ and $h=\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor$.
Depth one admits all $\tau(n)$ cells. At depth two, an interval containing
one heavy gap has mass $M+2\ell$, so the cutoff $W/2=M+n-1/2$ admits it
exactly for length $\ell\le h$. There are four disjoint sets of $h$ such
intervals, and $\tau(n-4)$ intervals avoiding both heavy gaps. Thus
\[
 S_2+4n=\tau(n-4)+4h+4n=\tau(n)+6+4h>\tau(n).
\]
For $d\ge3$ with $2^d\le n$, we have $3\le F_{d+1}\le2^d\le n$.
The cutoff is below $M$ but above every all-unit interval mass, so
$S_d=\tau(n-4)$ and
\[
 S_d+n2^d\ge\tau(n-4)+8n=\tau(n)+4n+6>\tau(n).
\]
If $2^d>n$, the top term alone exceeds $n^2\ge\tau(n)$. Every positive
depth is therefore worse than the direct Knuth plan. This is a quadratic
obstruction to the specified constructor, not a general OBST lower bound.
An independent contour check rejected the initial assumption that all
one-heavy-gap intervals were admitted at depth two; the correct length
cutoff above is retained in the test. The family motivates joint treatment
of several comparable heavy outcomes. Eight full instances through 256 keys
match Knuth and reconstructed costs, with cubic checks through 32 keys.
Four larger instances through 65,536 keys check plans only; at that size
the selected table has 2,147,516,416 cells, not a completed full solve.

\section{Joint heavy-outcome cores}\label{sec:heavy-core}
The two-gap obstruction motivates selecting several heavy outcomes together.
The following certificate uses their necessary comparison keys as an
ancestor-closed core. It preserves the complete mixed-weight objective and
solves every intervening interval exactly. Priority of the sufficient condition
has not been established.

For $n\ge1$, choose a nonempty set $\mathcal H$ of positive-weight key or gap outcomes.
Let $A$ contain every selected key and the adjacent key(s) of every selected
gap. Write $m=|A|$, $\theta=\min_{x\in\mathcal H}w_x$, and let $W_0$ be
the total weight outside $\mathcal H$. Both adjacent keys of an interior gap
are ancestors of that gap in every BST. Thus each $A$-key lies on the search path to
some selected outcome, possibly itself. Call a tree \emph{core-closed} if no key outside $A$
is an ancestor of an $A$-key. Then $A$ forms a top BST and the intervals
between consecutive $A$-keys, including both end intervals, are its payloads.
Selected gaps remain singleton core gaps.

\begin{lemma}[Projection preserving selected depths]\label{lem:core-projection}
Every BST can be reduced to a BST on $A$ without increasing any selected
outcome's depth. If the original tree is not core-closed, at least one selected
outcome becomes strictly shallower.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Consider an outside key $r$ whose subtree contains an $A$-key. If just one
child contains $A$-keys, replace $r$ by that child. The other branch contains
no selected outcome: any nonempty subtree containing a selected gap must
contain one of its neighboring keys, and a selected empty child gap would
make $r$ itself an $A$-key. All retained selected outcomes save a comparison.

If both children contain $A$-keys, let $a$ be the largest such key in the
left subtree. Replace $r$ by $a$, restrict the old left subtree to keys below
$a$ and their endpoint gaps, and retain the old right subtree. No selected
outcome lies strictly between $a$ and $r$: a selected key would belong to
$A$, and a selected gap would put an adjacent key in $A$ before or at $r$.
All $A$-keys survive. Selected outcomes in the left restriction lose zero or
more comparisons by Lemma~\ref{lem:restriction}; right depths are unchanged.
If $a$ is selected, it moves strictly upward. Otherwise it must border a
selected gap on its left. Its right gap cannot be selected, since that would
put $a+1$ in $A$, contradicting either the maximality of $a$ or $r\notin A$.
The selected left gap loses the comparison at the old $a$. Thus some selected
depth decreases in either case.

These exchanges may discard light keys, which will be reinserted later.
Each exchange reduces the number of outside keys and retains $A$. Repeat
until no outside key has an $A$-descendant, then discard the remaining
anchor-free branches. Selected gaps are preserved throughout. This produces
the asserted core tree. Arbitrary deletion of nonretained comparisons would
not suffice: a nonretained key can separate retained outcomes on both sides;
the explicit promotion above resolves that case.
\end{proof}

A first sufficient condition is $\theta\ge(m+L)W_0$, where
$L=\lceil\log_2(n+1)\rceil$. Project an optimum and reinsert balanced
payloads: the selected cost falls by at least $\theta$, and the new light
cost is at most $(m+L)W_0$. The next theorem substantially sharpens the
height term using the earlier Fibonacci lemma.

\begin{theorem}[Protected-height core certificate]\label{thm:heavy-core}
Let $W_{\mathcal H}$ be the selected total weight and
\[
 H=\max\{j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}:F_j\theta\le W_{\mathcal H}\},
 \qquad F_1=F_2=1.
\]
If
\begin{equation}\label{eq:heavy-core}
 \theta\ \ge\ (H+L)W_0,
\end{equation}
there exists a core-closed optimum. If the payload intervals have masses
$W_t$ and unrestricted relative optimum costs $C_t$, then
\[
 \OPT(\text{original})
 =\OPT(\text{original }A\text{-key weights},\ (W_t)_t)+\sum_t C_t.
\]
Equality in the certificate does not force every optimum to be core-closed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
In a protected-only core instance, keep selected weights and set every other
weight to zero. Every internal subtree contains an $A$-key and therefore
a selected outcome of weight at least $\theta$ in that subtree. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:mass}, a depth-$d$ internal subtree of a protected-only optimum
has mass at most $W_{\mathcal H}/F_{d+1}$. Its height, including external
leaves, is consequently at most $H$.

Project a non-core-closed full optimum by Lemma~\ref{lem:core-projection},
saving at least $\theta$ in selected weighted cost. Replace the projected
core by a protected-only optimum; its selected weighted cost cannot increase,
although individual depths may change. Reinsert balanced payloads. Every
unselected key comparison length or gap depth is at most $H+L$, so the new
light cost is at most $(H+L)W_0$. The old light cost was nonnegative.
Condition~\eqref{eq:heavy-core} makes the exchange nonincreasing, establishing
the existence of a core-closed optimum.

At core-gap depth $d$, an exact payload contributes $dW_t+C_t$. The core
and payload relative costs therefore optimize independently, proving the
identity. Empty intervals contribute zero relative cost and keep their gap
weight. The final full-weight core is optimized without a height restriction:
$H$ bounds the protected-only witness, not the output core.
\end{proof}

For $k$ equal selected weights, $H=O(\log(k+1))$, whereas $m$ can be $2k$.
The certificate thus handles much less extreme dominance than the size-based
version. It does not remove unselected weights from the final objective.

\paragraph{A threshold sweep with no repeated interval scans.}
The implementation selects all outcomes of weight at least a threshold
$\theta$. Key $i$ is in $A$ precisely when
$\max(p_i,q_i,q_{i+1})\ge\theta$. Sort these activation weights and all
outcome weights. Sweep thresholds upward, grouping equal values. Before
each threshold, activate as unanchored each key whose activation weight is
strictly smaller. A disjoint-set structure maintains the contiguous payload
runs and their total triangular state count. A singleton adds one; merging
runs of sizes $a,b$ adds $ab$. Union by size bounds each find by $O(\log n)$,
even without using an inverse-Ackermann analysis.

At each certified threshold, evaluate
\[
 \tau(m)+\sum_t\tau(k_t),\qquad \tau(k)=k(k+1)/2.
\]
Keep the minimum proxy, accepting only a strict improvement on $\tau(n)$.
This optimizes a Knuth-state proxy, not actual frontier runtime. Fibonacci
values through index $n$ take $O(n)$ additions. Since
$W_{\mathcal H}/\theta$ is nonincreasing during the upward sweep, a single
decreasing Fibonacci index supplies the height bound in $O(n)$ total index
decrements; cap it by $m$. The entire selection uses $O(n\log(n+1))$
arithmetic work and $O(n)$ arithmetic storage. Failure invokes the preceding
separator constructor, adding screening work without a failed quadratic solve.

\paragraph{Kernels, reconstruction, and scoped bounds.}
Both core and payloads use either exact local frontiers or Knuth. Before each
kernel, uniform key weights $p$ and uniform gap weights $q$ are recognized.
Such a tree costs $(p+q)I+np+2nq$, where $I$ is its unweighted internal path
length. A recursively balanced tree minimizes $I$ and is built in linear
work. This familiar uniform-weight shortcut is not claimed as new.

If $K(s)$ bounds the chosen exact kernel on $s$ keys, successful construction
costs
\[
 O\!\left(n\log(n+1)+K(m)+\sum_t K(k_t)\right),
 \qquad m+\sum_t k_t=n.
\]
Sequential scratch reuse retains only the payload outputs between solves.
The general work and space bounds remain quadratic. A uniform residual
background and $k$ selected outcomes give $m\le2k$ and work
$O(n\log(n+1)+k^2)$. If residual weights lie in $[a,Ca]$ for fixed $a>0$
and $C$, each payload's local frontier costs $O(s^\alpha)$, where
$\alpha=1+\log_{2\phi}2<1.59024$. A certified plan then costs
\[
 O\!\left(n\log(n+1)+k^2+\sum_t k_t^\alpha\right).
\]
With payload sizes at most $B$, the last sum is at most $nB^{\alpha-1}$.
These are bounds for the certified plan; minimizing the triangular proxy
need not minimize this sharper expression.

For the preceding obstruction, $q_1=q_{n-1}=n^3$ with unit background,
$n\ge5$, we have $m=4$, $H=3$, and $W_0=2n-1$. The certificate passes,
so the four-key core and uniform payloads take $O(n\log n)$ total work.
The previous separator/frontier constructor used all $n(n+1)/2$ cells.
Bounded positive mixed background and sufficiently large equal selected gap
weights instead give an $O(n^{1.59024})$ plan. This resolves that scoped
obstruction; it is not an unrestricted subquadratic theorem.

\section{Residual-cost certificates for heavy-outcome cores}\label{sec:core-exclusion}
The preceding height certificate charges all light search cost against one
saved heavy comparison. Every competing tree must itself pay for light
searches. Crediting a valid lower bound for that unavoidable cost gives a
stronger test for a fixed selected set. This is an additive exclusion
certificate built on Lemma~\ref{lem:core-projection}; it does not assert a new
Huffman algorithm or an established priority claim.

Write the original nonnegative weight vector as $w=w_H+w_0$, where $w_H$
retains selected positive outcomes and is zero elsewhere. Let $A$ be the
necessary core keys from Section~\ref{sec:heavy-core}, let $P$ be the exact
protected-only optimum on that core, and let $B_0$ be any valid lower bound
on the full $n$-key problem with residual weights $w_0$. In particular, the
order-free mixed-node relaxation of Section~\ref{sec:unordered} supplies
$B_0$. Residual weights on both keys and gaps are retained.

\begin{theorem}[Residual exclusion certificate]\label{thm:core-exclusion}
Every tree that is not core-closed has full cost at least
\[
 P+\theta+B_0,
 \qquad \theta=\min_{x\in\mathcal H}w_x.
\]
Consequently, if a core-closed witness has full cost $U\le P+\theta+B_0$,
some global optimum is core-closed. More strongly, if the exact optimum
$C_A$ within the core-closed class satisfies this inequality, then $C_A$
is the unrestricted optimum.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project any non-core-closed tree $T$ onto $A$. Its selected weighted cost
decreases by at least $\theta$, and the projected selected cost is at least
$P$. Thus $C_{w_H}(T)\ge P+\theta$. Independently,
$C_{w_0}(T)\ge B_0$. Add the inequalities, using linearity of tree cost
in the weights. A witness at or below this excluded-class lower bound
cannot be worse than every non-core-closed tree. Optimizing within the
core-closed class therefore gives a global optimum. Equality allows both
classes to contain optima.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{A test before solving the payloads.}
Solve the protected core, retaining one optimal shape, and attach balanced
payloads. This produces a full witness of cost $P+U_0$, where $U_0$ is its
exact residual cost. The inexpensive sufficient condition becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:residual-core}
 U_0-B_0\le\theta.
\end{equation}
Only after it passes does the default constructor solve the full-weight
core and all payloads exactly. Thus failed witnesses need no exact payload
tables. For the same selected set, this test includes the preceding height
certificate: $U_0\le(H+L)W_0$ and $B_0\ge0$. The implemented threshold
selection is not exhaustive, so this logical implication is not a claim
that the new selector always chooses the old selector's successful set.

An optional refinement solves one core-closed candidate even after its
witness fails and tests $C_A\le P+\theta+B_0$. For example,
$p=(0,0,1)$, $q=(0,4,1,1)$, and $\theta=4$ give $P=8$, $B_0=4$, and
excluded-class lower bound $16$. The first witness costs $17$, while the
exact closed candidate costs $14$ and is globally optimal. A failed witness
is not evidence that the core structure is invalid.

\paragraph{Several candidates without quadratic screening.}
A single minimum-proxy threshold can include ordinary outcomes whose
weights are too small to pay for the exchange. The revised selector keeps
two representatives in each dyadic core-size band: the least
$\tau(m)+\sum_t\tau(k_t)$ proxy and the highest threshold. It considers
only $m\le b=\lfloor\sqrt{n\lceil\log_2(n+1)\rceil}\rfloor$ and only
strict proxy improvements over $\tau(n)$. The existing sorted activation
sweep finds these representatives in $O(n\log(n+1))$ work. There are
$O(\log(n+1))$ candidates, and their squared core sizes sum to $O(b^2)$,
because at most two lie in each geometrically increasing band.

Sort the original key and gap profiles once. For a threshold $\theta$,
remove the profile entries at least $\theta$ and append the same number of
zeros. The remaining profile is still sorted. The mixed-node two-queue
bound then takes $O(n)$ work per candidate, without another sort. Building
and evaluating each balanced witness also takes $O(n+m^2)$ work. All
candidate screening therefore costs $O(n\log(n+1))$ arithmetic operations.
Candidates are tried in increasing proxy order until a witness succeeds.
If none succeeds, the optional mode refines only the first candidate;
limiting refinement to one avoids an $O(\log n)$ factor on failed exact
payload solves. Failure invokes the preceding exact constructor.

A successful plan retains the bound
\[
 O\!\left(n\log(n+1)+K(m)+\sum_t K(k_t)\right).
\]
Both modes keep quadratic general worst-case work and storage. The witness
screen can use $O(n\log n)$ storage for protected-core tables; the final
kernel can still require quadratic space. Ordinary Huffman coding is
classical~\cite{huffman}; the mixed-node relaxation and its limitations
are already described in Section~\ref{sec:unordered}. The result here is
the certified use of a residual lower bound against a specified excluded
class, rather than a relaxation substituted for the full objective.

\paragraph{A linear-weight separation from the height certificate.}
Let $n=2^h\ge8$, put every original key and gap weight equal to one, and
replace $q_1,q_{n-1}$ by an equal selected weight $\theta>1$. The necessary
core has four keys. Its protected optimum is $P=5\theta$: each selected
interior gap needs at least two comparisons, and both cannot have depth
two because their adjacent-key pairs are disjoint. Depths two and three
are attainable.

Use the core whose root is original key $1$, left child is key $0$, right
child is key $n-2$, and right-right child is key $n-1$. The sole nonempty
payload contains $n-4$ keys and hangs at depth two. The residual cost of
this balanced-payload witness is
\[
 U_0=(2h+3)n-6h-3.
\]
Indeed, the core's residual cost is $4n-1$, while
$I_{\min}(n-4)=(h-2)n-3h+5$ gives payload cost
$2I_{\min}(n-4)+3(n-4)=(2h-1)n-6h-2$.

For the residual problem all key weights are one, all ordinary gap weights
are one, and the two selected gaps have weight zero. The identity
$E=I+2n$ rewrites any residual tree cost as
$\sum_j(q_j+1)d_j-n$. The order-free leaf weights are therefore two ones
and $n-1$ twos. Huffman first merges the two ones; the resulting $n=2^h$
weights are all two and have a perfect code of height $h$. Hence the exact
order-free residual lower bound is
\[
 B_0=2nh-n+2,
 \qquad U_0-B_0=4n-6h-5.
\]
Thus $\theta\ge4n-6h-5$ suffices for core closure. In particular,
$\theta=4n$ passes, while the preceding height condition requires
$\theta\ge(h+4)(2n-1)$ and rejects it.

At $\theta=4n$, the described core is also optimal with the full collapsed
weights. The four protected-optimal shapes have residual core costs
$4n-1$ or $6n-9$; every other core shape has selected cost at least
$6\theta>5\theta+4n-1$. It follows that the full optimum is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:linear-gap-optimum}
 \OPT=(2h+23)n-6h-3.
\end{equation}
The new constructor uses a constant-size core and a uniform payload, for
$O(n\log n)$ work and $O(n)$ space on this strictly positive mixed-weight
family. These weights and the construction's intermediate values have
$O(\log n)$ bits. This is a separation from the preceding height
certificate, not a quadratic lower bound for its fallback frontier or
for other algorithms.

\section{Coupled two-gap skeleton certificates}\label{sec:pair-skeleton}
The preceding certificate adds independent protected and residual minima.
We now retain enough geometry to couple these costs at the first possible
violation. Select internal gaps $a<b$, with weights $M_1,M_2$, and let
$A=\{a-1,a,b-1,b\}$, $m=|A|\in\{3,4\}$, and
$\theta=\min(M_1,M_2)$. A tree is $A$-closed when no outside key is an
ancestor of an $A$-key. All weights below may be zero.

\begin{lemma}[First violating skeleton]\label{lem:pair-skeleton}
In a nonclosed tree the selected gap depth sum $D$ is at least $m+2$.
If $D=m+2$, the union of the two search paths has exactly $m+1$ keys:
the keys in $A$ and one outside key. The paths share only the root.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Each key neighboring a gap is an ancestor of that gap. Thus the path
union contains $A$. Nonclosure puts an outside ancestor of an $A$-key
in the same union. If $L\ge1$ keys are shared, its size is $D-L$, so
$D\ge m+1+L\ge m+2$. Equality forces $L=1$ and union size $m+1$.
\end{proof}

The one outside key is called the bridge. Its inorder position is in
one of the light intervals between keys of $A$. Enumerating binary-tree
shapes on $A$ and the bridge takes at most $C_5=42$ shapes per interval.
Only nonclosed shapes with selected depth sum $m+2$ are retained.

\begin{lemma}[Unordered mixed forests]\label{lem:mixed-forest}
Let $F_k(P,Q)$ be the minimum mixed cost of a $k$-root binary forest
with roots at depth zero, internal-weight multiset $P$, and leaf-weight
multiset $Q$, where $|Q|=|P|+k$. Weights may be permuted within each
type and empty trees are allowed. Sort $P$ ascending. For each weight
in that order, merge the two smallest current leaf weights with it,
add the resulting weight to the cost, and reinsert it as a leaf.
After all merges the accumulated cost is $F_k(P,Q)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose a deepest internal node in an optimal forest; it is a cherry.
Exchange weights to put a smallest internal weight there and the two
smallest leaf weights at its children, without increasing cost. Contract
the cherry to a leaf of their total weight. Its removed cost is exactly
that total. Conversely, expanding this leaf in any feasible contracted
forest has that added cost. Induction proves the recurrence, including
forests consisting only of leaves. Nonnegative weights ensure the generated
merge weights are nondecreasing. Two queues therefore take linear work
after sorting, for total $O(n\log n)$ work.
\end{proof}

Delete $A$ from inorder to obtain $m+1$ intervals. Two are the selected
singleton gaps. For every other region $s$, write its weights as
$(P_s,Q_s)$ and mass as $W_s$. In a retained skeleton, let $h_s$ be
its hole depth. An ordinary region contributes the lower bound
\[
 h_sW_s+F_1(P_s,Q_s).
\]
If the bridge is in region $t$, there are two holes. Lower their depths
to their minimum $h$. If the bridge comparison length is $\ell$, then
$h\ge\ell$, since both holes descend from it. All other internal
slots in this relaxed region have comparison length at least $h+1$.
Exchanging internal weights therefore places a largest regional weight
$x$ at the bridge. The relaxed contribution is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bridge-forest}
 \ell x+h(W_t-x)+F_2(P_t\setminus\{x\},Q_t).
\end{equation}
This exchange applies after lowering the two hole depths; it does not
identify the bridge key in the original ordered tree. Keep the exact
costs of all $A$-keys and both selected gaps. Minimizing the sum of
these costs and the regional bounds over retained skeletons gives
$B_{\rm first}$, a valid lower bound for $D=m+2$.

Let $B_0$ be the unordered mixed-tree optimum after zeroing the two
selected gap weights, retaining all other weights. Each internal gap
has depth at least two, so define
\begin{align*}
 B_{\rm basic}&=2(M_1+M_2)+(m-2)\theta+B_0,\\
 B_{\rm tail}&=2(M_1+M_2)+(m-1)\theta+B_0.
\end{align*}
The first bounds every nonclosed tree; the second bounds those with
$D\ge m+3$.

\begin{theorem}[Coupled exclusion certificate]\label{thm:pair-certificate}
Every nonclosed tree has cost at least
\[
 B=\max\{B_{\rm basic},\min(B_{\rm first},B_{\rm tail})\}.
\]
If the first class is empty, replace the inner minimum by $B_{\rm tail}$.
An $A$-closed witness of cost $U\le B$ certifies that some unrestricted
optimum is $A$-closed. Its exact construction then requires independent
exact solutions of the intervening intervals and a constant-size core.
The screening work is $O(n\log n)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:pair-skeleton} partitions excluded trees into the first
and tail classes. Each has its stated lower bound, and both have
$B_{\rm basic}$. The witness is no more costly than any excluded tree.
An optimum of the closed class is consequently globally optimal.
There are constantly many regions, forest computations, and skeletons;
Lemma~\ref{lem:mixed-forest} gives the screening bound.
\end{proof}

The implementation first uses midpoint payloads and an exact full-weight
core as the witness. Optional refinement computes the exact closed
candidate when this witness fails. An uncertified attempt falls back
to the preceding exact method; the general worst case remains quadratic.

\subsection{A family with arbitrary nonuniform key positions}
Constant key weights require a scope qualification. For $p_i=c$, the
internal/external path-length identity $E=I+2n$ gives
\[
 C(T;c,q)=\sum_g(q_g+c)d_T(g)-cn.
\]
Such inputs reduce to alphabetic leaf-weight problems. The next family
instead has a linear number of nonconstant key weights.

\begin{theorem}[Nonuniform-key family]\label{thm:nonuniform-pair}
Let $n=2^h$, $h\ge5$, $r=n/16$, and $s=r-1$. All gaps have weight
one except $q_1=q_{n-1}=M=3n$. All keys have weight one except any
$s$ keys in positions $2,\ldots,n-3$, which have weight two. The
midpoint witness passes Theorem~\ref{thm:pair-certificate} for every
placement of these keys. Combining it with the existing mass-frontier
construction takes $O(n^\alpha)$ arithmetic work, where
$\alpha=1+\log_{2\varphi}2<1.59024$.
For $h\ge6$, the earlier fixed-pair bound $B_{\rm basic}$ cannot
certify even the exact closed optimum on this family.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For unit keys write $U_0=(2h+3)n-6h-3$. The middle payload has $n-4$
keys, mass $2n-7$, and optimal relative cost $(2h-1)n-6h-2$.
The minimum four-key core has selected depth sum five, middle hole
depth two, and other contribution thirteen, giving cost $5M+4n-1$.
For completeness, the distinct other triples (selected depth sum,
middle depth, other contribution) among its 14 shapes are
$(5,3,12),(6,3,13),(6,3,15),(6,4,14),(7,4,13)$.
None is cheaper. Thus the unit-key closed optimum is $5M+U_0$.
A midpoint payload attains that baseline cost and has maximum relative
key comparison length $h$, so the nonuniform witness satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pair-witness}
 U\le 5M+U_0+(h+2)s.
\end{equation}

Let $G_k(t)$ be the minimum sum of comparison lengths of $t$ marked
internal nodes in a $k$-root forest. Filling the capacities
$k2^{\ell-1}$ at comparison lengths $\ell=1,2,\ldots$ gives
\[
 G_1(s)=(h-5)r+1,\qquad G_2(s-1)=(h-6)r+2.
\]
In the unit first-violation relaxation the middle two-root forest has
$n-5$ internal nodes. Its balanced profile has three leaves at depth
$h-2$ and $n-6$ at depth $h-1$. The forest identity
$E=I+2(n-5)$ yields
\[
 F_2=(2h-3)n-6h+5.
\]
There are ten admissible five-key skeleton profiles. Their distinct
triples (minimum middle hole depth $d$, bridge length $\ell$, fixed
core/endpoint contribution $c$) are
\[
 (2,2,16),\ (2,2,15),\ (3,3,13),\ (2,1,15),\ (3,1,14),\ (2,1,16).
\]
Their unit costs are $6M+2d(n-4)+\ell+c+F_2$; the minimum at
$(2,1,15)$ is
\[
 B_{\rm first}^{\rm unit}=6M+(2h+1)n-6h+5.
\]
With nonuniform keys the bridge takes weight two in the relaxation.
The remaining $s-1$ extra units receive hole shift at least two and
forest comparison lengths summing to at least $G_2(s-1)$. The bridge
adds at least one. Therefore
\[
 B_{\rm first}\ge B_{\rm first}^{\rm unit}+2s-1+G_2(s-1).
\]

The residual unordered optimum is exactly
\[
 B_0=2nh-n+2+G_1(s).
\]
For the unit baseline, absorbing the key weights leaves two leaf weights
one and $n-1$ weights two. Their Huffman tree is a perfect $h$-level tree
with one bottom leaf expanded into the two unit leaves; subtract $n$
to recover mixed cost $2nh-n+2$. Every tree pays at least $G_1(s)$
for the extra key units. The baseline optimal shape has its top $h-4$
internal levels full, so these same slots attain $G_1(s)$ simultaneously
in the unordered problem, establishing equality.

Substitute $M=3n$ in these bounds and in~\eqref{eq:pair-witness}:
\[
 B_{\rm first}-U\ge5n/8+h+9>0,\qquad
 B_{\rm tail}-U\ge25n/16+7h+8>0.
\]
The witness certifies every placement. The remaining middle interval
has key weights in $[1,2]$ and unit gap weights. Its bounded-positive
mass-frontier bound is $O(n^\alpha)$, and the $O(n\log n)$ screening
and full reconstruction fit within this bound. This exponent is inherited
from that architecture, not a new unrestricted exponent.

Finally, every closed tree costs at least its unit-background closed
optimum plus $G_1(s)$. Since $B_{\rm basic}=6M+B_0$, its deficit is
at least $n-6h-5>0$ for $h\ge6$. This is a separation from the earlier
certificate for this fixed pair, not a claim about every alternative
core its selector could attempt.
\end{proof}

The family's total mass is $O(n)$ and all key weights are at least one.
Consequently the earlier global mass frontier already has the same
$O(n^\alpha)$ guarantee on the entire instance. Theorem~\ref{thm:nonuniform-pair}
is a certificate separation with arbitrary nonuniform key placements,
not an extension of the known subquadratic input regime. Its practical
value must be judged by actual construction work and matched measurements.

\section{A useful relaxation that failed as a general solver}\label{sec:unordered}
Allow arbitrary assignment of key weights to internal positions and gap
weights to external positions. In this relaxed problem, repeatedly take
the two smallest current external weights $a,b$ and the smallest remaining
internal weight $p$, replace them by $a+b+p$, and add that sum to the cost.

To prove the rule, take a deepest internal node of an optimum.
Its children are leaves. Swapping the smallest internal weight into it
and the two smallest external weights into its children cannot increase
cost. Contract that cherry and induct. Sorting and a heap give
$O(n\log n)$ time. This elementary extension of the Huffman exchange
argument is not claimed as novel.

The relaxed cost is a valid lower bound for full OBST. Equality with a
feasible ordered tree certifies optimality. It agreed with an independent
shape-enumeration and rearrangement oracle on 22,143 exhaustive and 2,700
random instances in the basic harness.

However, using the relaxation on demand in an exact sparse DP was poor
on random inputs. At $n=128$ it visited roughly 4,300--4,600 exact intervals
instead of Knuth's 8,256, but spent approximately 3.9--4.1 million weight
comparisons constructing bounds and inspected over 100,000 candidate roots.
Fewer exact states did not mean less computation.

Nor does the relaxation's interval matrix inherit the required quadrangle
inequality. For $p=(9,1,12)$, $q=(5,1,18,10)$, its costs $H$ satisfy
\[
 H(1,2)+H(2,3)=60>54=H(1,3)+H(2,2).
\]
Thus applying Knuth-style matrix optimization directly to this bound
would be unjustified.

Two additional exact counterexamples exclude tempting local certificates.
With $p=(3,5,18)$, $q=(13,14,4,8)$, the tree with root 2 and left subtree
$0(\varnothing,1)$ costs 127, every single-rotation neighbor costs at least 134,
yet the balanced tree costs 125.
With $p=(18,8,18)$, $q=(17,11,1,13)$, the balanced tree costs 164 and every
distinct tree obtained by promoting a descendant to the root of an existing
subtree costs at least 169; the tree $0(\varnothing,2(1,\varnothing))$ costs 157.
These are barriers to those local certificates, not complexity lower bounds.

\section{An ordered depth-chain lower bound}
Let $l_i$ be the comparison length of key $i$ and $g_i$ the depth of gap $i$.
For every valid BST, adjacent keys have different lengths and
\[
 g_0=l_0,\quad g_n=l_{n-1},\quad
 g_i=\max(l_{i-1},l_i)\quad(0<i<n).
\]
This identity is existing work; see Becker~\cite{becker}, Lemma~1.
Let $K_d$ count keys of length $d$ and $G_d$ count gaps at depth $d$.
The root and child-count identities are
\[
 K_1=1,\qquad G_d+K_{d+1}-2K_d=0\quad(1\le d\le n),
 \qquad K_{n+1}=0.
\]

Permit any sequence in $\{1,\ldots,n\}^n$ with unequal adjacent entries.
For arbitrary multipliers $\lambda_0,\ldots,\lambda_n$, minimize
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dual}
 \sum_i p_i l_i+\sum_i q_i g_i
 +\lambda_0(K_1-1)
 +\sum_{d=1}^n\lambda_d(G_d+K_{d+1}-2K_d).
\end{equation}
Call the minimum $L(\lambda)$. Every actual tree is permitted and has zero
penalty, so $L(\lambda)\le\OPT$ for every multiplier vector.
The expression is a chain with key energy
$U_i(b)=p_i b+\lambda_{b-1}-2\lambda_b$, gap energy
$V_i(b)=q_i b+\lambda_b$, and constant $-\lambda_0$.
For an interior transition into length $b$, take the smaller of
\[
 \min_{a<b}F(a)+V_i(b),\qquad
 \min_{a>b}\{F(a)+V_i(a)\}.
\]
Prefix and suffix minima give $O(H)$ work per key for a domain of $H$ lengths.
Using $H=n$ would cost quadratic time even for one bound evaluation.

\begin{theorem}[Exact affine-tail compression]
Fix $h\ge1$, arbitrary $\lambda_0,\ldots,\lambda_h$, and a slope
$0\le s\le\min_i p_i$. Extend
$\lambda_d=\lambda_h+s(d-h)$ for $d>h$.
The minimum in~\eqref{eq:dual} is unchanged if lengths are restricted to
$1,\ldots,\min(n,h+3)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For $b\ge h+1$, the unary key energy has slope $p_i-s\ge0$ and the gap
energy has slope $q_i+s\ge0$. Both are nondecreasing in this tail.
If some $l_i>h+3$, replace it by a member of
$\{h+1,h+2,h+3\}$ unequal to its current neighbors. At most two values
are forbidden. The key energy does not increase. Each adjacent maximum
is either unchanged or decreases within the affine tail, so neither gap
energy increases. Endpoint gaps obey the same argument. Repeat for all
large lengths. The restricted domain is a subset of the original domain,
and every original sequence has a no-more-expensive restricted sequence;
therefore the minima are equal.
\end{proof}

Taking $h=\lceil\log_2(n+1)\rceil+2$ gives an $O(n\log n)$ oracle.
This is a statement about minimization of the relaxed energy, with no height
assumption on the unknown optimal BST. Optimizing only the head multipliers
requires folding all tail constraint violations into the derivative for
$\lambda_h$. The implementation uses exact integer multipliers and costs
scaled by 1024. Its subgradient ascent is heuristic; the bound is valid
at every iteration regardless of convergence.

A stronger oracle requires exactly one length1 key. For each candidate
root, the left and right chains then contain only lengths at least2.
One forward and one reverse pass supply their closing costs for every
root position, giving all forced-root bounds in $O(nH)$ total time.
The projection above preserves the unique root, so tail compression remains
valid for each marginal. Retaining the maximum bound seen for each root,
then taking the minimum over roots, is also a valid lower bound.

Both oracles passed 50 comparisons against exhaustive length-sequence
minimization, 5,000 arbitrary-multiplier bound checks against cubic OBST,
and 1,050 direct comparisons of the full and shortened oracles.
Forced-root marginals were additionally checked against exact
root-constrained costs.

\paragraph{A structural gap.}
The sequence $l=(3,1,2,3,2)$ has
$g=(3,3,2,3,3,2)$, a unique root, and all the level-count equalities.
It is not a valid BST depth sequence. For
\[
 p=(3,27,21,16,12),\qquad q=(18,22,27,22,12,22),
\]
it costs 470, while the true optimum is 472. Its penalty is zero for every
multiplier vector. Hence neither oracle family can ever exceed 470 on
this instance. More optimization iterations cannot remove this obstruction.

\paragraph{Preliminary strength and cost.}
At $n=256$, normalized gaps $(\OPT-\mathrm{LB})/W$ for the chain bound
versus order-free Huffman were 0.15003 versus 0.22080 on a random mixed
instance, and 0.00873 versus 0.49294 on an alternating instance.
However, 300 ascent iterations used 1,082,368 key-depth cells including
initialization, compared with 32,896 interval states for Knuth.
The unique-root variant uses two passes and still leaves 254 of 256
potentially improving roots on the random instance, and 255 on the
alternating instance. Only two survive on the gap-spike instance.
These results identify a lower-bound component and its limitations;
they do not establish a faster general exact solver.

\section{Exact depth ordering and an arbitrary-window obstruction}
Call a positive integer sequence \emph{ranked} if every nonempty contiguous
interval has a unique minimum. Equivalently, equal labels have a smaller
label between them. This is vertex ranking on a path with the label order
reversed; vertex ranking and bounded-path variants are existing
notions~\cite{ranking,localranking}. The following results explain the
ordering information absent from the preceding relaxation; no novelty claim
is made for this characterization.

\begin{theorem}[Depth characterization]
A sequence $l\in\{1,\ldots,n\}^n$, $n\ge1$, is the inorder
key-comparison-length sequence of a BST if and only if it is ranked,
$K_1=1$, and every level-flow equality holds, with gaps derived from $l$
as above.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Necessity follows from the unique shallowest key in each inorder interval
and the two children of every internal node. For sufficiency, delete all
labels at most $d$. Let $U_d$ count the remaining nonempty runs, and let
$A_d$ count the deleted labels. If the deleted labels form $R$ runs and
include $e$ endpoints, then the number of gaps of length at most $d$ is
$A_d-R+e$, whereas $U_d=R+1-e$. Thus
\[
 G_{\le d}=A_d+1-U_d.
\]
Summing the level-flow equalities from 1 through $d$, and using $K_1=1$,
also gives
\[
 G_{\le d}=A_d+1-K_{d+1}.
\]
Hence $U_d=K_{d+1}$. Ranking permits at most one label $d+1$ per remaining
run, so equality forces exactly one. Starting with the unique length1
root, every remaining nonempty interval therefore receives its root at
the next level. This constructs the tree with exactly the supplied lengths.
\end{proof}

Ranking has a linear recognizer: scan with an increasing stack, pop larger
labels, reject an equal top, and otherwise push. A rejected pair has no
smaller intervening label; conversely every such pair causes rejection.
Each entry is pushed once and popped at most once. This establishes cheap
recognition, not cheap minimization over all valid sequences.

\begin{theorem}[Bounded-window obstruction]
For every integer $w\ge2$ and $t\ge2$, there is a strictly positive full-OBST
instance with $n=w+3$ keys and an inadmissible depth sequence satisfying
all level-flow equalities, a unique length1 root, and ranking on every
interval of at most $w$ keys. Its cost is at most
$(7/8+1/(8t))\OPT$. Consequently every lower bound obtained by pricing
the level-flow equalities over this window-constrained domain has the same
upper limitation, regardless of the multipliers.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Take
\[
 l=(3,1,2,3,4,\ldots,w+1,2).
\]
The ascending middle portion has $w-1$ entries. Its two enclosing length2
labels form the only interval with a repeated minimum, of width $w+1$.
Every shorter window passes. Direct substitution into the gap formula
verifies $K_1=1$ and every level-flow equality. The sequence is invalid:
the unique root is key1, but its singleton left subtree has length3,
and two keys to its right both claim length2.

Give every gap weight1 and every key weight1 except
\[
 p_1=3B,\qquad p_2=p_{n-1}=B.
\]
The unit-weight outcomes contribute
$E=w^2+4w+10$ in the inadmissible sequence, whose cost is $7B+E$.
Every actual tree costs at least $8B$. If key1 is the root, the two
$B$-weight keys lie to its right and their comparison lengths sum to at
least5, giving $3B+5B$. Otherwise key1 has length at least2 and each
$B$-weight key has length at least1, giving $6B+2B$.

Set $B=tE$. The inadmissible cost satisfies
\[
 7B+E=\left(\frac78+\frac{1}{8t}\right)8B
       \le\left(\frac78+\frac{1}{8t}\right)\OPT.
\]
Its level-flow penalty is zero for every multiplier vector, so every
priced minimum is at most this cost.
\end{proof}

For $t=16$ the upper ratio is $113/128$, independently of $w$; as $t$
increases it approaches $7/8$. This rules out universal exactness of the
specified window-minimum repair, not other local constraints, adaptive
nonlocal cuts, or subquadratic full-OBST algorithms.
Equivalently, at every $n\ge5$ the construction passes all windows of up
to $n-3$ keys.

\paragraph{Burying the obstruction below a forced prefix.}
Let a current instance have mass $W$ and a feasible tree of cost $U$.
Prepend a key of weight $A=W+U+2$ and a new leftmost gap of weight1.
Using the new key as root costs $A+1+W+U=2A-1$, while every other root
costs at least $2A$ from that key alone. Hence the new root is forced and
$\OPT_{\rm new}=A+1+W+\OPT_{\rm old}$.
The transformation $l\mapsto(1,l_0+1,\ldots,l_{n-1}+1)$ preserves zero
flow and local window ranking, and changes the inadmissible cost by the
same $A+1+W$. Repeating this $D$ times places the strict gap beneath the
true optimal top-$D$ right-chain prefix. Thus fixing any constant number
of top levels cannot make the remaining window/flow relaxation exact.
The constant relative gap is not claimed after this embedding, because
the forcing weights add a potentially large common cost.

\paragraph{Independent verification.}
The harness enumerated all 873,612 sequences in $\{1,\ldots,n\}^n$ for
$1\le n\le7$. The characterization accepted exactly the independently
generated BST depth sequences: respectively 1, 2, 5, 14, 42, 132, and 429.
The stack recognizer agreed with direct interval scans on every sequence.
Width3 and exact flow already admit four invalid sequences at $n=6$ and
34 at $n=7$. The positive family was checked at $t=16$ for all 127 widths
$2\le w\le128$: exact costs, window validity, zero flow, and the tree-cost
lower bound all passed. Knuth and direct tree evaluation checked every
instance; independent cubic DP also checked $w\le24$.
Another 120 cases combine $2\le w\le16$ with forced prefix depths
$1\le D\le8$. Cubic DP, Knuth, direct costs, and inspection of the optimal
prefix verify the exact additive identity and preserved strict gap.

\section{Endpoint responses: insufficiency and representation size}
An intact subtree interacts with its ancestors through its relative cost
and total mass. If its interior is not yet fixed, one tempting richer
summary is the response to added weights at its endpoint gaps:
\[
 \mathcal R(x,y)=\OPT(p,q+xe_0+ye_n),\qquad x,y\ge0.
\]
For a fixed tree the response is $C(T)+xd_0(T)+yd_n(T)$, so
$\mathcal R$ is a concave lower envelope of affine planes. We distinguish
whether this function contains enough information for composition from
how many pieces an explicit representation requires. Neither result below
is a general lower bound for optimal-BST construction; priority remains
unestablished.

\subsection{Equal responses need not be interchangeable}
\begin{theorem}[Insufficient endpoint-value interface]\label{thm:response-insufficient}
An interval's endpoint response, length, total mass, and endpoint gap
weights do not determine its optimal cost in an arbitrary surrounding
search problem. This remains true if its full vector of key weights
and its multiset of gap weights are also retained.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Consider two four-key intervals with
\[
 p=(1,1,2,3),\quad q^A=(1,4,2,1,1),\quad q^B=(1,4,1,2,1).
\]
Both have mass 16 and endpoint gap weights one. Enumerating the 14
four-key shapes gives the following least intercepts for each possible
endpoint-depth pair:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{crr}
\toprule
Depths & $A$ & $B$\\
\midrule
$(1,2)$ & 45 & 45\\
$(1,3)$ & 42 & 42\\
$(1,4)$ & 44 & 45\\
$(2,1)$ & 43 & 43\\
$(2,2)$ & 36 & 36\\
$(2,3)$ & 36 & 37\\
$(3,1)$ & 39 & 39\\
$(3,2)$ & 36 & 36\\
$(4,1)$ & 43 & 42\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Each plane with slopes $(1,4),(2,3),(3,2)$, or $(4,1)$ is pointwise
dominated on the nonnegative quadrant by a common plane whose slopes
and intercept are no larger. Hence both responses are exactly
\begin{align*}
 \mathcal R(x,y)=\min\{&45+x+2y,\ 42+x+3y,\ 43+2x+y,\\
                      &36+2x+2y,\ 39+3x+y\}.
\end{align*}
This proves equality for all parameters, not just sampled ones.

Prepend keys of weights $(7,1)$, append keys of weights $(1,7)$, and
give the four new gaps weight one. The full inputs are
\begin{align*}
 P&=(7,1,1,1,2,3,1,7),\\
 Q^A&=(1,1,1,4,2,1,1,1,1),\\
 Q^B&=(1,1,1,4,1,2,1,1,1).
\end{align*}
Using the exact recurrence, the least full costs conditional on each
root are
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{lrrrrrrrr}
\toprule
Root & 0 & 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 & 5 & 6 & 7\\
\midrule
$A$ & 108 & 108 & 103 & 101 & 101 & 101 & 111 & 110\\
$B$ & 108 & 108 & 103 & 101 & 100 & 101 & 111 & 110\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Thus the optima are 101 and 100. Direct enumeration of all 1,430
eight-key trees independently verifies both rows. Equal summaries
with the identical surrounding context therefore have unequal answers.
\end{proof}

In particular, exact prepend/append updates closed on the stated summary
cannot exist: identical starting summaries and identical updates would
remain indistinguishable. The theorem does not exclude extra shape
information, non-exposed depth states, signed-price responses, or richer
interfaces. It does not assert a distinction between mixed and alphabetic
algorithms; it rejects this summary even on small positive mixed inputs.

The lost information includes internal alternatives with identical boundary
depths. Both intervals have minimum cost 36 at depths $(2,2)$. The shape
rooted at key 2, with left child 0 (whose right child is 1) and right
child 3, also has depths $(2,2)$, but costs 37 in $A$ and 36 in $B$.
The least intercept erases this distinction; the surrounding tree can
make the additional alternative valuable.

\subsection{Quadratic explicit envelopes with linear factorizations}
For any $n\ge1$, the endpoint paths share only the root. If their depths
are $a,b$, their union has $a+b-1\le n$ keys. At most one least
intercept per depth pair is needed, giving at most $n(n+1)/2$ affine
pieces. The quadratic order can occur already for a single interval.

\begin{theorem}[Explicit-facet obstruction]\label{thm:response-facets}
There are strictly positive mixed-weight instances on $n=2m+1$ keys
whose endpoint response has at least $m^2$ affine pieces on the
nonnegative quadrant. Consequently, explicitly listing all such pieces
can require $\Omega(n^2)$ entries.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Set $K=4m$ and $P_i=K8^i$ for $0\le i<m$. First consider these $m$
keys with all gaps one and added left endpoint weight $x$. Write
$S_j=\sum_{i=0}^jP_i$ and $x_j=2S_j+2m$.

An endpoint key and its adjoining outer gap are forced to the root
if their combined weight exceeds the mass of all other outcomes.
Indeed, each rotation of that key toward the root saves one comparison
on both outcomes and adds at most one to each other outcome. The strict
inequality makes every rotation improve cost. At $x_j$, for every $k>j$,
\[
 P_k+1>S_{k-1}+x_j+k+1,
\]
while in the remaining prefix
\[
 P_0+1+x_j>(S_j-P_0)+(j+1).
\]
The geometric series and $K=4m$ imply these inequalities. For example,
$S_{k-1}<P_k/7$ and $x_j\le2S_{k-1}+2m$ for $k>j$, whereas
$P_k\ge32m$. Thus every optimum first uses roots
$m-1,m-2,\ldots,j+1$, then key 0, giving left endpoint depth $m-j$.
All $m$ possible one-sided slopes occur, with strict inequalities
persisting in open neighborhoods of the probes.

Mirror this instance about a central key:
\[
 p=(P_0,\ldots,P_{m-1},R,P_{m-1},\ldots,P_0),\quad q=(1,\ldots,1).
\]
Let $X=x_{m-1}$, $S=S_{m-1}$,
$B=2S+2m+2+2X$, and $R=(2m+2)B$.
For $0\le x,y\le X$, the mass outside the central key is at most $B$.
A center-root tree costs at most $R+nB<2R$; any other root charges
the central key at least $2R$. Thus the center root is forced.
At $(x_j,x_k)$, every optimum has endpoint depths
\[
 (m-j+1,m-k+1).
\]
These are $m^2$ different gradients. Since there are finitely many
trees and all minimizing trees at each probe have the same gradient,
that probe has an open affine neighborhood. Different gradients require
different pieces.
\end{proof}

The weights have $O(n)$ bits each. This is an arithmetic/output-size
obstruction to explicit facet enumeration, not a word-RAM lower bound
with $O(\log n)$-bit weights, nor a lower bound for a single optimum query.
The same example also explains why counting facets cannot rule out
implicit representations.

\begin{lemma}[Exact hinge factorization]\label{lem:response-hinges}
For the one-sided family above, let
\[
 C_0=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}(m-i)P_i+m+\frac{m(m+1)}2.
\]
Its response for every $x\ge0$ is
\[
 H_m(x)=C_0+mx-\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\max\{0,x-(P_i-P_0)\}.
\]
Within the forced-center box the two-sided response equals
\[
 R+2S+2m+2+x+y+H_m(x)+H_m(y).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
At $x_j$, the remaining right subtree of key 0 is a decreasing-key
chain, by the same strict endpoint condition. Passing from $j$ to
$j+1$ rotates key 0 above key $j+1$. At base weights the intercept
increases by $P_{j+1}-P_0$, while the endpoint slope decreases by one.
At $j=0$ the intercept is $C_0$. Every possible endpoint depth occurs
among these $m$ trees, and optimality at a probe proves its intercept
is minimal for that depth. Their successive intersections are the
increasing thresholds $P_i-P_0$, giving the hinge formula. The center-root
recurrence gives the two-sided identity.
\end{proof}

The factorization extends to the entire quadrant. Set
\begin{align*}
 W&=R+2S+2m+2, & V&=W-P_0-1,\\
 L&=C_0-mP_0-m-1, & J&=R+2(S-P_0)+2m+2L.
\end{align*}
Here $L$ is the fixed cost of the remaining one-sided keys
$1,\ldots,m-1$, and $J$ is the fixed inner cost after both outer keys
and outer gaps are removed.

\begin{theorem}[Full-quadrant factorization]\label{thm:response-global}
For the geometric family and every $x,y\ge0$,
\begin{align*}
 \mathcal R(x,y)=W+x+y+\min\{&H_m(x)+H_m(y),\\
 &V+y+L+H_m(y),\ V+y+J,\\
 &V+x+L+H_m(x),\ V+x+J\}.
\end{align*}
This function has exactly $m^2+2m+2$ affine pieces. It admits $O(m)$
arithmetic preprocessing and storage, $O(\log m)$ value queries, and
$O(m)$ complete tree construction per query.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Any root other than keys $0,m,2m$ gives both added endpoint weights
depth at least two and the central key comparison length at least two.
A center-root candidate with the two outer keys as children also puts
both endpoints at depth two, with base cost at most
$R+n(W-R)<2R$. It therefore strictly beats such a root for all $x,y$.
If root 0 is used, its remaining one-sided problem similarly has only
the center or the other outer key as a possible root; symmetrically for
root $2m$. Expanding these five cases gives the formula. The fixed
remaining intervals are geometric chains on either side of a forced
central root, giving the stated $L,J$.

The center-root term supplies $m^2$ slopes, all exposed by the original
probes. For root 0, the same $2R$ argument forces the center in the
right problem throughout the original probe range, exposing all $m$
pieces inherited from $H_m(y)$. As $y$ grows unbounded, one final piece
appears when the outer key becomes its root. Taking $x$
sufficiently large forces root 0, exposing these $m+1$ pieces globally.
Their slopes are $(1,2),\ldots,(1,m+2)$. Reflection gives another
$m+1$ pieces with second slope one. All slopes are distinct. The formula
has no further candidate slopes, proving the count.

Prefix sums of the ordered thresholds let two binary searches evaluate
$H_m(x)$ and $H_m(y)$. Four comparisons select the outer minimum.
The selected case and one-sided chain indices reconstruct
exactly $2m+1$ keys in linear work.
\end{proof}

Thus $2(m-1)$ hinge terms and five alternatives represent a quadratic
number of affine regions. Endpoint-dominance shortcuts already solve this
family. It is not a hard-query example, and the facet theorem is not an
expression-size lower bound. A lossless factorization also does not repair
Theorem~\ref{thm:response-insufficient}: the value function itself omits
information needed by some surrounding contexts.

\subsection{A favorable bound independent of the added weights}
\begin{lemma}[Endpoint mass-ratio bound]\label{lem:response-ratio}
Let the original total mass be $W$, with $q_0,q_n>0$. The entire response
for arbitrary $x,y\ge0$ has
\[
 O\!\left(\log(2+W/q_0)\log(2+W/q_n)\right)
\]
affine pieces, also bounded by $O(n^2)$. Polynomial endpoint mass ratios
therefore give $O(\log^2 n)$ pieces.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If an optimum has left endpoint depth $a\ge2$, its last internal
ancestor is at depth $a-2$ in the root's left subtree. This subtree
is optimal and excludes the opposite endpoint and its added weight $y$.
Its mass is at most $W+x$, while the last ancestor's subtree has mass
at least $q_0+x$. Lemma~\ref{lem:mass} gives
\[
 F_{a-1}\le\frac{W+x}{q_0+x}\le\frac{W}{q_0},\qquad F_1=F_2=1.
\]
The symmetric bound is $F_{b-1}\le W/q_n$ for right depth $b\ge2$.
Fibonacci growth bounds the number of possible slopes in each direction;
only a least intercept per pair can be required.
\end{proof}

The added parameters do not occur in this bound. The quadratic example
has exponential original mass ratio, so the two results are consistent.
This lemma bounds output size, not the work to discover the pieces.
Moreover, the composition counterexample has just five pieces: a small
response need not be a sufficient interface.

The exact-arithmetic harness checks 875 response queries against cubic DP

\section{A core-sparse Monge reduction with terminal penalties}
The preceding interface counterexample motivates preserving the actual
interval costs in compressed form. We now give a reduction to the published
core-sparse Monge product of Gawrychowski, Gorbachev, and
Kociumaka~\cite{coremonge}. It yields a smaller exponent, up to logarithms,
for linear total integer mass than the frontier bound above, and permits
zero weights. Dense reference audits and condensed Python/C++ constructors
check the reduction. The compact backend is slower than matched Knuth
construction at tested sizes; priority of this application is unestablished.

Let $n\ge1$, let all key and gap weights be nonnegative integers, and put
$W=\sum p_i+\sum q_i$ and $L=\lceil\log_2(n+1)\rceil$. Write $f(W)$ for
the first index with $F_{f(W)}>W$, where $F_0=0,F_1=1$. Set
\[
 H=\min\{n,f(W)+L\},\qquad M=(H+1)W+1.
\]
If every key weight is positive, $H=\min\{n,f(W)\}$ suffices.

\begin{theorem}[Integer-mass sparse-product reduction]\label{thm:penalty-reduction}
Using the core-sparse Monge multiplication and witness algorithms
of~\cite{coremonge}, a full optimal BST can be constructed in
\[
 O\!\left(H\left[n+\min\{n^2,n\sqrt{WL}\}\right]\log(n+1)\right)
\]
arithmetic operations. Retaining the witness structures uses
$O(H[n+\min\{n^2,n\sqrt{WL}\}])$ words. In particular, $W=O(n)$ gives
$O(n^{3/2}\log^{5/2}(n+1))$ exact construction. Intermediate integers have
$O(\log(n+1)+\log(W+1))$ bits.
\end{theorem}
The dependence on numerical mass is essential to this statement. The
linear-mass case fits ordinary $O(\log n)$-bit arithmetic, but arbitrary
large integer weights do not give an unrestricted strongly subquadratic
bound. Knuth remains a fallback when the displayed bound is unfavorable.

\subsection{An upper iteration that finishes in a bounded number of levels}
Initialize $A_0(i,j)=M(j-i)$ for $i\le j$, and iterate
\begin{align*}
 A_h(i,i)&=0,\\
 A_h(i,j)&=W(i,j)+\min_{i\le r<j}
       \{A_{h-1}(i,r)+A_{h-1}(r+1,j)\}\quad(i<j).
\end{align*}
This is an $h$-level partial search tree: explicit decisions incur the usual
interval mass, while each key still in an unfinished terminal interval
incurs penalty $M$. If $t$ keys are explicit, the cost is
\[
 M((j-i)-t)+D,\qquad 0\le D\le hW(i,j).
\]
At most $\min\{j-i,2^h-1\}$ keys can be explicit, and that number is
attainable with inorder labels. Since $M>HW$, every minimizer for
$h\le H$ uses that maximum number. Writing $c_h=2^h-1$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:penalty-residual}
 A_h(i,j)=M\max\{0,j-i-c_h\}+R_h(i,j),\qquad
 0\le R_h(i,j)\le hW(i,j).
\end{equation}
The residual $R_h$ is not asserted to be Monge.

The initial bound dominates every interval optimum: any complete tree on
$j-i$ keys costs at most $(j-i)W(i,j)\le M(j-i)$. The recurrence is
monotone, and $A_1\le A_0$ because $W(i,j)\le M$. Thus the iterates
decrease and remain at least $C$. Whenever an optimal full tree fits
within $h$ levels, it is feasible without a terminal penalty, so $A_h=C$.

The mixed Fibonacci mass lemma applies here to an \emph{actual optimal
full tree}. Every positive-mass internal subtree at depth $d$ satisfies
$F_{d+1}\le W$. Zero-mass subtrees can be replaced by balanced trees of
height at most $L$ without changing cost. Therefore some optimum has
height at most $f(W)+L$; the trivial bound $n$ also applies. When all keys
have positive integer weight every internal subtree has mass at least one,
so the extra $L$ is unnecessary. These arguments apply to every interval,
proving $A_H=C$ throughout the upper triangle.

An earlier proposed iteration started from the additive singleton lower
bound. Its attempted rotation proof of logarithmic convergence was invalid:
a rotation can lift an unfinished interval, requiring extra refinement to
restore the prescribed number of levels. Small passing tests do not remove
that issue. The upper-penalty proof above avoids that argument.

\subsection{Finite Monge products, including the diagonal boundary}
For a triangular matrix $A$ with zero diagonal, define its interior density by
\[
 \Delta_A(i,j)=A(i,j+1)+A(i+1,j)-A(i,j)-A(i+1,j+1)
 \quad(0\le i<j<n).
\]
A \emph{core} lists nonzero densities. To use a finite matrix product, put
$P=4nM+4W+1$ and complete each layer by
\[
 E_h(i,j)=\begin{cases}A_h(i,j),&i\le j,\\P(i-j),&i>j.\end{cases}
\]
We prove by induction that $E_h$ is Monge. This is immediate for $E_0$.
Given the previous completion, multiply its first $n$ columns by its last
$n$ rows in the min-plus sense:
\[
 B_h(i,j)=\min_{0\le r<n}
       \{E_{h-1}(i,r)+E_{h-1}(r+1,j)\}.
\]
For $i<j$, every invalid root has a term at least $P$, whereas a valid
pair costs at most $nM$. Thus the finite product gives the restricted root
minimum exactly. The product is Monge. Adding $W(i,j)$, a row term plus
a column term, leaves density unchanged in squares entirely above the
diagonal. The only interior squares affected by resetting a diagonal entry
to zero have $j=i+1$. Their new density is
\[
 \min\{A_{h-1}(i,i+1),A_{h-1}(i+1,i+2)\}-q_{i+1}\ge0.
\]
For the first step both old singleton entries are $M$; thereafter each is
its singleton interval mass. The completion adds exactly the $n$ diagonal
densities $P+A_h(i,i+1)$; all densities below them vanish. This proves
Monge preservation, including the boundary cases. All entries are integers.

\subsection{A bound on every intermediate core}
Let $K_h$ be the number of nonzero interior densities of $A_h$.
A direct telescoping identity is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:core-mass}
 \sum_{i<j}\Delta_A(i,j)=A(0,n)-\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}A(i,i+1).
\end{equation}
For $h\ge L$, a balanced full tree is feasible, so $A_h(0,n)\le LW$.
Nonnegative integrality then gives $K_h\le LW$. This final-stage argument
alone would not bound the early layers, whose terminal penalties are large.

For $1\le h<L$, the penalty term in~\eqref{eq:penalty-residual} has
density only on the line $j-i=c_h$. Account for that line and the main
diagonal separately, using $O(n)$ cells. Tile the remaining density-index
triangle by maximal dyadic squares that do not cross either line. A
power-of-two enclosing square, also split at the outer domain boundary,
handles arbitrary $n$. At side length $s$ there are $O(n/s)$ tiles:
each parent of a maximal tile intersects one of a constant number of
straight boundaries, and each boundary meets $O(n/s)$ grid squares at
that scale.

On a tile $[a,b)\times[c,d)$ the penalty density vanishes. Its density
sum telescopes to
\[
 R_h(a,d)+R_h(b,c)-R_h(a,c)-R_h(b,d)\le2hW.
\]
All densities in this tile are nonnegative integers. Negative residual
densities can occur on the removed line and are not used. The number
of nonzero entries in the tile is at most $\min\{s^2,2hW\}$. Therefore
\[
 K_h=O\!\left(n+\sum_{s\text{ dyadic}}\frac ns
                         \min\{s^2,2hW\}\right)
     =O(n+n\sqrt{hW}),
\]
capped by $O(n^2)$. Splitting the geometric sum at $s\asymp\sqrt{hW}$
introduces no extra logarithm. For $W=0$, only the removed-line entries
can remain. Combining the early and late bounds, including the $n$
completion entries, yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:penalty-core-bound}
 \delta(E_h)=O\!\left(n+\min\{n^2,n\sqrt{WL}\}\right)
 \qquad(0\le h\le H).
\end{equation}
This controls representation size without charging the large value of $M$
as though it represented many separate core entries.

\subsection{Compact computation and full reconstruction}
Theorem~1.2 of~\cite{coremonge} multiplies finite Monge matrices from
their condensed representations in time linear in their dimensions plus
input core size times its logarithm. Theorem~4.7 supplies compact witness
oracles in the same time. Fact~4.1 provides logarithmic-time entry queries
after core-based preprocessing. These are existing algorithms, not results
claimed here.

Use them on the two contiguous submatrices of $E_{h-1}$. To obtain the
next completion, retain the product core strictly above the first
superdiagonal, where adding interval mass has zero density. Recompute the
$O(n)$ diagonal and first-superdiagonal core entries, discard lower entries,
and recover the top row and left column. Filtering and $O(n)$ logarithmic
entry queries take $O((n+\delta)\log(n+1))$ work, including coordinate
sorting if needed. The product's temporary core is also bounded linearly
by the two input cores, by Theorem~1.1 of~\cite{coremonge}. No step
first enumerates a dense interval table.

Retain the product witness oracle for every layer. Starting from $(0,n,H)$,
retrieve a root and recurse on its two intervals at layer $H-1$.
There are $n$ nonempty calls. An unfinished nonempty interval at layer zero
would incur at least $M$, but the optimum is at most $LW<M$, since
$H\ge L$. Thus none survives; when $H=n$, completeness also follows from
depth alone. Witness recovery costs an additional $O(n\log(n+1))$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:penalty-core-bound}, summed over $H$ layers, proves
Theorem~\ref{thm:penalty-reduction}, including dimensions, representation
conversion, storage, and reconstruction. Since $H\le n$ and the penalties
are polynomial in $n,W$, the stated bit-length bound follows as well.

\subsection{Implemented condensed constructors}
The compact path is now implemented in Python and independently in C++.
The product algorithm is the published compression, balanced core split,
staircase merge, and decompression method of~\cite{coremonge}. We credit
that primitive separately from the OBST reduction. A matrix stores its top
row, left column, and positive density triples. Two linear bucket passes
canonicalize the triples. Boundary extraction uses one-dimensional sweeps;
the product merge uses adjacent-entry recomputation along its staircase.
Each relevant core row or column is scanned only a constant number of times
per merge. None of these operations enumerates the full interval table.

\paragraph{Exact early stopping.}
The constructors compare consecutive completed layers by their top rows
and canonical core triples; the left column is fixed. Equality is therefore
equality of the whole table. A fixed point satisfies the ordinary OBST
recurrence with zero diagonal. Induction on interval length proves that
this recurrence has a unique solution, namely $C$. Stopping at such a fixed
point is exact. Equality of the single full-interval cost is not used.
Retained layer witnesses reconstruct a complete optimum: any unfinished
nonempty terminal would contribute at least $M>HW$, exceeding the
cost of an optimal tree of height at most $H$. All charges are nonnegative.
Reconstruction uses an explicit stack and visits every original key.

\paragraph{Implementation costs and arithmetic.}
Both implementations retain ordinary integer coordinate maps and recursive
witness nodes. Their witness storage is bounded by
$O(H(n+K)\log(n+1))$ words, where
$K=O(n+\min\{n^2,n\sqrt{WL}\})$ bounds the completed cores. This can

size, and the core count halves along a recursive branch, so the time bound
is preserved. No succinct rank/select implementation is claimed.

The C++ variant stops product recursion at leaves with at most 64 output

\]
Validation precedes both timed constructors. These are prototype-driver
limits, not restrictions of the theorem. The benchmark executable separately
runs Knuth, which allocates a dense table.

\section{A centered core bound for large integer baselines}
The total-mass bound can be pessimistic when large constant key and gap
weights dominate smaller variations. We retain the original weights in the
solver and center only the analysis of its matrix representation. Let
\[
 a=\min_i p_i,\quad b=\min_j q_j,\quad
 u_i=p_i-a,\quad v_j=q_j-b,\quad R=\sum_i u_i+\sum_j v_j.
\]
All quantities remain nonnegative integers. The two baselines may differ
arbitrarily; the deviations may occur at arbitrary key and gap positions.
Write $L=\lceil\log_2(n+1)\rceil$ as before.

\begin{lemma}[Normalized height budget]\label{lem:normalized-height}
For $W>0$, let $s$ be the smallest positive singleton mass
$p_i+q_i+q_{i+1}$. Put $z=0$ if every singleton mass is positive and $z=L$
otherwise. Every interval has an optimal tree with height at most
\[
 H=\min\{n,f(\lfloor W/s\rfloor)+z\}.
\]
For $W=0$, use $H=L$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Every positive-mass nonempty interval contains a positive-mass singleton:
choose a positive key, or an adjacent key within the interval if a gap
supplies its positive mass. Thus every positive internal subtree has mass
at least $s$. Lemma~8 gives $W\ge F_{d+1}s$ for such a subtree at depth $d$
in an optimal tree. The positive part has the resulting bounded depth;
zero-mass subtrees can be balanced, adding at most $L$ levels without
changing the cost. If all singleton masses are positive no nonempty zero
subtree exists. The global $W,s,L$ bounds also apply to every subinterval.
The trivial height $n$ and the all-zero balanced case complete the argument.
\end{proof}

Use this $H$, $M=(H+1)W+1$, and the same finite completion and penalty
iteration. All previous preservation arguments apply. Reconstruction is
complete because any unfinished nonempty terminal costs at least $M$,
whereas an optimal tree of height at most $H$ costs at most $HW$.

\subsection{The exact baseline layer and its exceptional diagonals}
Let $I(k)$ be the minimum unweighted internal path length for $k$ keys.
For $k\ge1$, with $d=\lfloor\log_2 k\rfloor$,
\[
 I(k)=dk-2^{d+1}+d+2,\qquad I(0)=0.
\]
The familiar baseline identity is
$(a+b)I(T)+(a+2b)k$ for a full $k$-key tree. It was already used by the
minimum-path-length certificate; the following application concerns every
partial-tree layer.

\begin{lemma}[Centered partial-tree formula]\label{lem:centered-layer}
With $c_h=2^h-1$, define
\[
 F_h(k)=
 \begin{cases}
 (a+b)I(k)+(a+2b)k,&k\le c_h,\\
 M(k-c_h)+(a+b)h(k+1)-ac_h,&k>c_h.
 \end{cases}
\]
Then the full-weight layer admits
\[
 A_h(i,j)=F_h(j-i)+U_h(i,j),\qquad
 0\le U_h(i,j)\le hR(i,j),
\]
where $R(i,j)=\sum_{i\le r<j}u_r+\sum_{i\le r\le j}v_r$.
The baseline's density at interval length $k\ge1$ is
\[
 F_h(k+1)+F_h(k-1)-2F_h(k)=
 \begin{cases}
 a+b,&k=2^t-1,\quad 1\le t<h,\\
 M-b,&k=2^h-1,\\
 0,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The penalty forces exactly $\min(k,c_h)$ explicit keys. If $k\le c_h$,
a full minimum-path-length tree fits in $h$ levels. Otherwise a partial
tree exposing $c_h$ keys must fill every position in the first $h$ levels.
Its explicit keys have baseline cost
$a\sum_{t=0}^{h-1}(t+1)2^t$; unfinished keys cost $ah(k-c_h)$, and all
$k+1$ gaps cost $bh(k+1)$. This gives the second branch of $F_h$.
Nonnegative deviations cannot reduce the baseline optimum. Conversely,
a baseline-optimal partial tree charges every deviation at most $h$ times,
proving the residual bounds. The displayed densities follow by taking
second differences of the two branches. In particular, changes in the
full-tree slope occur only at $k=2^t-1$.
\end{proof}

The residual $U_h$ need not be Monge. The actual layer is Monge, and on
any square whose density cells avoid the exceptional diagonals, its density
equals the residual density. Baseline magnitudes do not increase the number
of exceptional diagonals.

\subsection{A weighted packing argument}
Remove all density cells on $j-i=2^t-1$ within the domain, and the main
diagonal, accounting for $O(nL)$ cells. Tile the remainder with maximal
aligned dyadic squares that avoid those lines and the outer boundary.
For a side-$s$ tile $T=[x,x+s)\times[y,y+s)$, let
$J_T=[x,y+s]$ be its spanning input interval, including both gap endpoints.

There are $O(nL/s)$ tiles at scale $s$: every parent meets a removed line
or an outer boundary, and each line meets $O(n/s)$ aligned parent squares.
A stronger weighted statement is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:centered-cover}
 \sum_{T:\,\mathrm{side}(T)=s} R(J_T)=O(nR/s).
\end{equation}
It has no factor $L$. To prove it, fix an input position and charge each
tile to a boundary met by its parent. A tile charged to a diagonal at
distance $d\ge s$ lies within $O(s)$ of that diagonal. For its spanning
interval to contain the fixed position, its row start lies in an interval
of length $O(d+s)$. Row starts are spaced by $s$, with only $O(1)$ eligible
column starts per row near that diagonal. Thus it contributes
$O(1+d/s)$ covers. The distances $d=2^t-1$ are geometric, so summing these
bounds gives $O(n/s)$. All distances $d<s$ lie together in an $O(s)$-wide
strip near the main diagonal and contribute only $O(1)$ covers of the fixed
position. Outer-boundary tiles number $O(n/s)$ in total. Charging each
nonnegative deviation to its covering intervals proves~\eqref{eq:centered-cover}.
This argument handles non-power-of-two domains by including their outer
boundaries in the same subdivision.

On an ordinary tile the density sum telescopes to
\[
 U_h(x,y+s)+U_h(x+s,y)-U_h(x,y)-U_h(x+s,y+s)
 \le 2hR(J_T).
\]
The true densities are nonnegative integers. The number of nonzero entries
on a tile is therefore bounded both by $s^2$ and by that density sum.
Using the tile count and~\eqref{eq:centered-cover}, the number across all
tiles of side $s$ is $O(\min\{nLs,hnR/s\})$. Splitting the dyadic geometric
sum at $s\asymp\sqrt{hR/L}$ gives $O(n\sqrt{hRL})$; for $R=0$ every
ordinary density vanishes. No cancellation of negative residual densities
on the removed lines is assumed.

\begin{theorem}[Centered core construction]\label{thm:centered-core}
For arbitrary nonnegative integer mixed weights, the normalized-height
penalty construction has completed layer cores bounded by
\[
 K_c=O\!\left(n+\min\{n^2,nL+n\sqrt{HRL}\}\right).
\]
Using the published compact product and witness algorithms, a complete
optimum can be constructed in $O(HK_c\log(n+1))$ arithmetic operations.
Published succinct witnesses use $O(HK_c)$ words; the implemented ordinary
maps can require $O(HK_c\log(n+1))$ words.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Add the $O(nL)$ removed cells and $n$ completion densities to the ordinary
tile bound, and use $h\le H$. The initial layer has only $O(n)$ core entries.
The quadratic cap is immediate. All products, conversion steps, and root
queries operate on the actual condensed matrices, so the already established
product reduction applies. The previous $O(n+n\sqrt{WL})$ bound is also
available; either bound may be used when smaller.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Arbitrarily large baselines with polynomial deviation]
If $R$ is polynomial in $n$, exact full construction takes
\[
 O\!\left(n(1+\sqrt R)\log^3(n+1)\right)
\]
arithmetic operations, independently of the magnitudes of $a,b$ in the
operation count. In particular, $R=O(n)$ gives
$O(n^{3/2}\log^3(n+1))$, and $R=O(n^{2-\varepsilon})$ gives
$O(n^{2-\varepsilon/2}\log^3(n+1))$ for fixed $0<\varepsilon\le2$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
If $a+b=0$, then $W=R$, and $s\ge1$ whenever $W>0$. Otherwise every
singleton has mass at least $a+2b\ge1$, and
\[
 W=na+(n+1)b+R\le n(a+2b)+R,\qquad W/s\le n+R.
\]
Thus $H=O(\log(n+R+1))=O(\log(n+1))$ for polynomial $R$, including the
possible additional $L$ and the all-zero case. Substituting in the theorem
gives the result.
\end{proof}

For example, $p_i=n^c+u_i$ and $q_j=n^d+v_j$, with fixed nonnegative
integer $c,d$ and bounded nonnegative integer deviations, satisfy the
linear-$R$ condition even when $W$ is superlinear. Intermediate integers
then still use $O(\log n)$ bits. Arbitrary larger baselines retain a bit-cost
dependence on their representation lengths. These families permit nonuniform
weights on both keys and gaps. This extends this project's core-size theorem;
it is not a claim that all such families lacked restricted subquadratic
algorithms before this work. The earlier total-mass result has a smaller
logarithmic factor when $W=O(n)$. Mathematical priority is unestablished.

\section{Ruler patterns and a dense-core representation barrier}
\label{sec:ruler}
The geometric-weight control has a dense core whose entries are constant
along each row. This motivates asking whether another finite difference
could restore sparse support. The answer is negative even for a smooth,
positive, polynomial-weight family that has an exact compact formula.
Before proving that barrier, we isolate the ruler pattern underlying both
the penalty layers and a new sufficient exchange condition. Priority of
these identities and conditions is unestablished.

\subsection{Saturated layers as weighted subsequences}
Write $c_h=2^h-1$ and let $\nu_2(t)$ be the exponent of two dividing
the positive integer $t$. Use the penalty recurrence above with $M>HW$.
For any interval of length $k=j-i\ge c_h$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ruler-layer}
 A_h(i,j)=M(k-c_h)+hW(i,j)
 -\max_{i\le s_1<\cdots<s_{c_h}<j}
       \sum_{t=1}^{c_h}\nu_2(t)p_{s_t}.
\end{equation}
Indeed, the penalty forces exactly $c_h$ exposed keys: losing one exposure
costs more than any possible reduction in the ordinary weighted cost.
Every position in the first $h$ levels is occupied. The exposed skeleton is
perfect, all gaps and unexposed keys pay $h$ comparisons, and its $t$th
inorder key has comparison length $h-\nu_2(t)$. This proves the identity.
The coefficient sequence is $0,1,0,2,0,1,0,3,\ldots$.

At $h=2$ the maximum is simply $\max_{i+1\le r\le j-2}p_r$.
At $h=3$, greedy selection of the largest eligible root fails: for
$p=(0,9,0,9,10,9,0,0)$ and all nine gap weights equal to one, root~3
permits score~36, while the larger key at root~4 permits only~29.
Adding one to all key weights adds four to every feasible score, giving
the same failure with strictly positive weights. For nondecreasing $p$,
the rightmost $c_h$ keys maximize the score coordinatewise; for
nonincreasing $p$, the leftmost keys do. Thus their saturated-layer density
vanishes whenever $j-i\ge c_h+1$, independently of the gap weights.
This does not discard the gaps or solve the full problem: shorter intervals
have nonuniform gap depths, and their band becomes quadratic at large $h$.
The weighted-subsequence reformulation by itself supplies no new efficient
min-plus product.

\subsection{A sufficient exchange condition for a wide last level}
Let $h=\lfloor\log_2(n+1)\rfloor$, $c=2^h-1$, and $s=n-c$.
As proved earlier, minimum internal-path-length shapes expand $s$ of the
$2^h$ external slots of a perfect $c$-key skeleton. Consider adjacent
skeleton slots $g,g+1$ containing one expansion. If their first input
index is $i$, moving the expansion from the second slot to the first
changes the weighted cost by exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ruler-exchange}
 (1+\nu_2(g+1))(p_i-p_{i+1})+q_i-q_{i+2}.
\end{equation}
Only the intervening skeleton key, added key, and three gaps change their
comparison lengths; all later positions are unchanged. The coefficient
$1+\nu_2(g+1)$ lies between one and $h$.

\begin{samepage}
\begin{theorem}[Exchange-certified minimum-path-length shape]
If
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ruler-condition}
 \max\{p_i-p_{i+1},\ h(p_i-p_{i+1})\}\le q_{i+2}-q_i
 \quad(0\le i<n-1),
\end{equation}
then filling the last level from the left minimizes weighted cost among
all minimum internal-path-length shapes. The reflected condition gives
the corresponding right-filled shape. For $s=0$ the shape is unique
without any condition.
\end{theorem}
\end{samepage}
\begin{proof}
The maximum in~\eqref{eq:ruler-condition} bounds the key term
in~\eqref{eq:ruler-exchange}, whether its difference is positive or
negative. Every adjacent swap of expansion pattern $01$ to $10$ is
therefore nonincreasing in cost. Repeated swaps move every expansion
leftward, yielding the claimed shape. Reflection proves the other case.
\end{proof}

In particular, nondecreasing key and gap weights satisfy the condition;
both lists nonincreasing satisfy its reflection. Nonmonotone keys are also
permitted when the gap increments compensate for their decreases.
This proves optimality \emph{within the shape class}. Global optimality
still requires the earlier residual-Huffman gate. For candidate value $V$,
minimum weights $a,b$, and residual mixed-node lower bound $H_{\rm res}$,
the sufficient condition is
\[
 V\le(a+b)(I_{\min}(n)+1)+(a+2b)n+H_{\rm res}.
\]
The implementation \texttt{ordered\_complete.py} constructs the candidate,
checks this gate, and otherwise falls back to Knuth. Accepted instances
take $O(n\log n)$ time and $O(n)$ auxiliary space, including sorting for
the bound. If both weight lists are monotone, scans/reversal and the
existing two queues make the whole accepted route $O(n)$. No optional-slot
width appears. Sorted geometric controls pass the shape condition but fail
the global gate, preventing an incorrect general claim about balanced trees.

\subsection{A positive family with dense differences and compact costs}
Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ruler-family}
 B=4(n+1)^3,\qquad p_r=B+r\ (0\le r<n),\qquad
 q_r=B+r\ (0\le r\le n).
\end{equation}
All weights use $O(\log n)$ bits and the deviation above their minimum
baselines is exactly $R=n^2$.

\begin{theorem}[Dense support does not force expensive construction]
For the family~\eqref{eq:ruler-family}, the optimal interval-cost matrix
has $\Omega(n^2)$ nonzero Monge densities and $\Omega(n^2)$ nonzero mixed
fourth differences. It also has quadratically many rowwise density changes.
Nevertheless, its exact tree is constructible in $O(n)$ time, and any
interval value can be queried using $O(\log n)$ arithmetic operations
after linear recognition of the family.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
First force the shape class. An interval of $k$ keys starting at $i$ has
constant baseline $B+i$. A tree of internal path length $I$ has total key
and gap comparison length $D=2I+3k\le k^2+2k$. All relative weights lie
between zero and $k$, so their total contribution is at most
$n(n^2+2n)$. Increasing $I$ by one adds at least $2B$, which exceeds this
entire bound. Hence every optimum has minimum $I$. Its adjacent exchange
cost~\eqref{eq:ruler-exchange} is $-3-\nu_2(g+1)<0$, so its last level
is filled from the left.

Here is an exact value formula. For $k=j-i>0$ put
$h=\lfloor\log_2(k+1)\rfloor$, $c=2^h-1$, $s=k-c$, and define
\begin{align*}
 I(k)&=(h-2)2^h+2+hs, & D(k)&=2I(k)+3k,\\
 J(c)&=\sum_{t=1}^c(t-1)\nu_2(t), &
 T(c,s)&=\sum_{t=1}^c\min\{s,t\}\nu_2(t),\\
 E(k)&=hc^2-J(c)+s(2hc+h+1)\\
     &\hspace{1em}+(h+3)s(s-1)-T(c,s).
\end{align*}
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ruler-formula}
 C(i,j)=(B+i)D(k)+E(k),\qquad C(i,i)=0.
\end{equation}
To verify $E$, the perfect skeleton has linear-weight cost $hc^2-J(c)$.
For an arbitrary set $S$ of expanded slots, each skeleton key at $r$
shifts by the number of selected slots at most $r$, and each skeleton gap
at $g$ shifts by the number strictly below $g$. Summing these index changes
and the new key/gap contributions gives
\begin{align*}
 E(S)={}&hc^2-J(c)+\frac{(2h+3)|S|(|S|-1)}2\\
 &+\sum_{g\in S}\left(2hc+3g+h+1-
                        \sum_{t=g+1}^c\nu_2(t)\right).
\end{align*}
Taking $S=\{0,\ldots,s-1\}$ gives~\eqref{eq:ruler-formula}.
For each $\ell=1,\ldots,h-1$, let $d=2^\ell$,
$m=\lfloor c/d\rfloor$, and $u=\min\{m,\lfloor s/d\rfloor\}$.
The contributions to $J$ and $T$ are, respectively,
\[
 \frac{dm(m+1)}2-m,\qquad
 \frac{du(u+1)}2+(m-u)s.
\]
This evaluates the formula in $O(\log n)$ steps, using
$\nu_2(t)=\sum_{\ell\ge1}[2^\ell\mid t]$. The tree itself needs one
recursive visit per key: its left subtree has
$2^{h-1}-1+\min\{s,2^{h-1}\}$ keys. Recognition scans the given input;
the interval oracle then retains only $n$ and $B$.

Now examine support. Write
\[
 d(i,j)=C(i,j+1)+C(i+1,j)-C(i,j)-C(i+1,j+1).
\]
Inside a fixed $h$ band, for $1\le s<2^h$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ruler-density}
 d(i,j)=3+\nu_2(s),\qquad s=(j-i)-(2^h-1).
\end{equation}
Indeed, $D$ has slope $2h+3$, while $E$ has second difference
$2h+6+\nu_2(s)$. The endpoint at $s=2^h$ is the next perfect tree, so
the calculation remains valid for $s=2^h-1$.
For $2\le s\le2^h-2$ the mixed fourth difference is therefore
\begin{align}
 &d(i,j+1)+d(i+1,j)-d(i,j)-d(i+1,j+1)\notag\\
 &\hspace{3em}=\nu_2(s+1)+\nu_2(s-1)-2\nu_2(s).\label{eq:ruler-fourth}
\end{align}
It is strictly positive for odd $s$ and strictly negative for even $s$.
For $n=2^m\ge32$, choose $h=m-2$ and $2\le s\le n/4-2$.
There are $n/4-3$ lengths and at least $n/2+2$ valid placements per length,
giving at least $(n/4-3)(n/2+2)=\Omega(n^2)$ nonzeros. For arbitrary
sufficiently large $n$, use $h=\lfloor\log_2 n\rfloor-2$; both the band
width and number of placements remain $\Theta(n)$. The same bands prove
quadratic second-difference support and rowwise density changes.
\end{proof}

Consequently any method explicitly listing either support has quadratic
output work on this family. This is a representation lower bound; the
linear constructor rules out interpreting it as a computational lower bound
for the family. The example also reaches the $R=\Theta(n^2)$ boundary of
the centered analysis with smooth, very nearly equal relative weights.
Symbolically representing the dyadic baseline avoids the dense list, but
subtracting it need not leave a Monge residual. Efficient product closure
with arbitrary residuals is still missing.

\section{Certified pruning around affine weight profiles}
\label{sec:ramp-corridor}
The dense ruler example suggests retaining its structure symbolically.
Here it bounds the states of a shape DP instead of the entries of a Monge
matrix. Perturbations may change the optimal tree; we optimize over all
retained shapes and separately certify global optimality. The result covers
arbitrary signed perturbations on both keys and gaps, under explicit size
conditions. The unrestricted question and priority remain open.

\subsection{An affine potential and a signed residual bound}
Let $h=\lfloor\log_2(n+1)\rfloor$, $S=2^h$, $s=n-(S-1)$, and $z=S-s$.
A minimum internal-path-length shape expands $s$ of $S$ skeleton slots.
Let $T_0$ expand the first $s$ slots. Choose nonnegative slopes
$\lambda,\mu$ and reference weights $\bar p_i=\lambda i$,
$\bar q_i=\mu i$. Constant intercepts contribute equally to every shape
in this class and can be omitted. Define
\[
 \gamma=\lambda+2\mu,\qquad
 F(g)=(2\lambda+2\mu)g-\lambda\operatorname{popcount}(g).
\]
By~\eqref{eq:ruler-exchange}, moving an expansion one slot right costs
$\gamma+\lambda\nu_2(g+1)=F(g+1)-F(g)$. Thus the affine cost of a
selected slot set $A$, relative to $T_0$, is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:affine-regret}
 G(A)=\sum_{g\in A}F(g)-\sum_{g=0}^{s-1}F(g).
\end{equation}
For $\gamma>0$, it is at least $\gamma$ times the number of unexpanded
slots preceding expanded slots.

The analytical residuals $\delta_p=p-\bar p$, $\delta_q=q-\bar q$ may
be signed. Actual problem weights remain nonnegative. All minimum-path-length
shapes have the same key and gap depth multisets. Assigning the largest
residual weights to the shallowest lengths, separately by type, gives a
valid rearrangement lower bound even for signed residuals. With descending
sorts indicated by $\downarrow$, it is
\begin{align*}
 L_\delta={}&\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\delta_{p,i}^{\downarrow}
                          \operatorname{bitlength}(i+1)
       +h\sum_i\delta_{q,i}\\
       &+\text{sum of the smallest $2s$ gap residuals}.
\end{align*}
The last term is zero for $s=0$. Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:affine-gain}
 V=C_\delta(T_0)-L_\delta\ge0.
\end{equation}
For a class optimum $T^*$, comparison with $T_0$ gives
\[
 G(T^*)\le C_\delta(T_0)-C_\delta(T^*)\le V.
\]
Adding a constant to all residuals of either type changes both terms in
\eqref{eq:affine-gain} equally. The bound is therefore independent of
intercepts and takes $O(n\log n)$ work including sorting.

\subsection{A certified corridor with few states}
At a slot-prefix state let $a$ be the number of zeros already used and
$b$ the number of ones still needed, with $0\le a\le z$, $0\le b\le s$.
Increasing $F$ makes the earliest available slots optimal on each side of
this prefix. The exact minimum affine regret of any completion through it is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:affine-state}
 K(a,b)=\sum_{t=s-b}^{s-1}\bigl(F(t+a)-F(t)\bigr).
\end{equation}
For $P(t)=\sum_{g=0}^{t-1}F(g)$, this is
$P(s+a)-P(s-b+a)-P(s)+P(s-b)$, computable in constant time after a
linear prefix pass. Every prefix of $T^*$ satisfies $K(a,b)\le V$.
Delete the others and run the existing two-transition shape DP using the
\emph{original} weights. The bound preserves a class optimum; unnecessarily
retained paths are still feasible original shapes.

\begin{theorem}[Affine-regret corridor]
For $\gamma>0$, put $D=\lfloor V/\gamma\rfloor$. Exact optimization
over minimum internal-path-length shapes, including preprocessing and
reconstruction, takes
\[
 O\!\left(n\log(n+1)+\min\{n^2,D\log(n+1)\}\right)
\]
arithmetic work and
$O(n+\min\{n^2,D\log(n+1)\})$ auxiliary space.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Each term in~\eqref{eq:affine-state} is at least $\gamma a$, so a retained
state must satisfy $ab\le D$. The two boundary segments have $S+1$ states;
the total is bounded by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:affine-state-count}
 S+1+\sum_{a=1}^z\min\{s,\lfloor D/a\rfloor\}
 \le S+1+\min\{sz,D\operatorname{bitlength}(z)\}.
\end{equation}
Group the harmonic sum into dyadic blocks for the last inequality.
Popcounts and $P$ are computed in a linear pass, each state has at most
two transitions, and stored predecessors reconstruct all keys. Sorting
for~\eqref{eq:affine-gain} adds $O(n\log n)$ work.
\end{proof}

For $\gamma=0$ the safe bound is the full shape grid. If $V=0$, the
reference is already class-optimal; a perfect skeleton also bypasses DP.
The implementation tries both orientations and uses the smaller certified
state bound. The default full solver rejects a bound exceeding
$n\operatorname{bitlength}(n)^2$ before starting the shape DP.
An unbudgeted class optimizer remains available for the theorem above.

Global optimality still requires the mixed residual-Huffman gate. It also
provides an exact preflight rejection: let $L_{\rm class}$ be the same
order-free fixed-profile lower bound on the original weights and
$L_{\rm out}$ the gate's lower bound for every nonminimum-path-length tree.
If $L_{\rm class}>L_{\rm out}$, no class candidate can satisfy the gate.
The solver goes directly to Knuth before fitting or shape optimization.
This rejects an impossible certificate, not the possibility that a class
tree is optimal. When preflight passes, its sorted lists and lower bound
are reused. An unfiltered mode is retained as a performance control.

\subsection{Robust fitting and full mixed-weight guarantees}
For a list of length $N$, let $m=\lfloor N/2\rfloor$. Take the median of
the disjoint secants $w_{i+m}-w_i$, $0\le i<m$, divide by $m$, round to
the nearest integer, and clip at zero. Sorting costs $O(n\log n)$.
If $w_i=A+\lambda i+u_i$ with integer $\lambda\ge0$, the sum of secant
errors is at most $E=\sum|u_i|$. Hence its median differs from $\lambda m$
by at most $E/\lceil m/2\rceil$. The odd unused endpoint remains in every
cost and certificate. Fitting quality controls the bound's usefulness;
arbitrary fitted slopes still give valid pruning inequalities.

\begin{theorem}[Arbitrary perturbations of a positive linear profile]
Let $n\ge32$, $B=4(n+1)^3$, and
\[
 p_i=B+i+u_i,\qquad q_i=B+i+v_i,
\]
with integer perturbations. The implemented full constructor has the
following guarantees, including inference of slopes, the global certificate,
and complete reconstruction:
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $\sum|u_i|+\sum|v_i|\le n$, time and auxiliary space are
      $O(n\log^2(n+1))$.
\item If $|u_i|\le1$ and $|v_i|\le1$ independently at every position,
      time and auxiliary space are $O(n\log(n+1))$.
\end{enumerate}
Both types of weights are positive and may be nonmonotone. The returned
tree is globally optimal in the full mixed-weight problem.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For the first case the forward fits recover $\lambda=\mu=1$:
$E/(m\lceil m/2\rceil)<1/2$ for either list at these sizes. Key lengths
range from one to $h+1$ and gap lengths from $h$ to $h+1$, even in the
relaxed profile assignment. Writing $E_p=\sum|u_i|$ and $E_q=\sum|v_i|$,
\[
 V\le hE_p+E_q\le hn,\qquad \gamma=3.
\]
Equation~\eqref{eq:affine-state-count} gives $O(n\log^2 n)$ states and
a bound below the default budget. Choosing the better orientation cannot
increase that bound.

For the second case every secant error is at most two, so the forward
slopes are again recovered. Let $\rho_p,\rho_q\le2$ be residual ranges.
The total key depth deficit from $h+1$ is
\[
 Q=n(h+1)-(I_{\min}(n)+n)=2S-h-2.
\]
The profile's possible key gain is at most $\rho_p Q$. For gaps it is at
most $\rho_q\min\{2s,z\}$, using either the deeper set or its complement.
Thus $V<6S$, $D<2S$, and~\eqref{eq:affine-state-count} gives $O(n\log n)$
states, again below the default budget for $n\ge32$.

It remains to certify unrestricted optimality in both cases. Put
$a_0=\min p$, $b_0=\min q$, and
$R_0=\sum(p-a_0)+\sum(q-b_0)$. In the first case,
\[
 a_0+b_0\ge2B-n,\qquad
 R_0\le n^2+(n+1)E_p+(n+2)E_q\le2n^2+2n.
\]
In the second, $a_0+b_0\ge2B-2$ and
$R_0\le n^2+4n+2\le2n^2+2n$. The residual cost of any class candidate is
at most
\[
 (h+1)R_0\le2n(n+1)^2<2B-n.
\]
Every tree outside the class pays at least one extra unit of internal
path length, whose constant-baseline penalty is $a_0+b_0$. Therefore the
existing global gate passes even with zero residual lower bound. In
particular, preflight cannot reject these candidates. The computed class
optimum is a full optimum. All weights and intermediate costs have
$O(\log n)$ bits on these families, so the arithmetic bounds also hold
in a word RAM with $O(\log n)$-bit words.
\end{proof}

These guarantees allow perturbations at arbitrary positions and do not
require the reference tree to remain optimal. For example, at $n=24$,
$B=62{,}500$, $p_i=B+i+(-1)^i$, $q_i=B+i$, the left-filled tree costs
13,252,388. Moving its expansion from slot~7 to slot~9 uses two adjacent
exchanges and reduces the cost to the exact optimum 13,252,387. The previous
one-direction exchange certificate rejects this input; the corridor DP
finds and certifies the better tree.

\section{Exact transfers for joint key/gap signatures}\label{sec:run-shapes}
The affine corridor needs a favorable reference slope. The following
representation instead compresses constant runs of a local mixed-weight
signature, allowing arbitrary values between blocks and no small-residual
assumption. Full optimality still requires a separate certificate.

\subsection{A skeleton word and its exact reward}
For $n\ge1$, let $h=\lfloor\log_2(n+1)\rfloor$, $S=2^h$, $m=S-1$,
and $s=n-m$. Encode a minimum-path-length shape by a word on its inorder
keys: a skeleton key is one and an added bottom cherry is zero. Exactly
the words with $m$ ones and no adjacent zeros are feasible. Initial and
terminal zeros are allowed. Each zero selects a distinct bottom slot,
so this is a bijection with the $\binom Ss$ slot subsets.

The skeleton key of rank $r$ among the ones has comparison length
$h-\nu_2(r)$; a cherry has length $h+1$. A gap has length $h+1$ exactly
when adjacent to a cherry, and otherwise length $h$. Put
$c_i=q_i+q_{i+1}$. Since zeros are nonadjacent, no deep gap is counted
twice. The full original cost of a feasible word $x$ is therefore
\begin{align}
 C(x)&=A-\sum_{i:x_i=1}\bigl((1+\nu_2(r_i))p_i+c_i\bigr),
       \label{eq:run-word-cost}\\
 A&=(h+1)\sum p_i+h\sum q_i+\sum c_i.\nonumber
\end{align}
Both weight types remain in this identity. It does not apply to arbitrary
taller shapes.

Partition $(p_i,c_i)$ into its $k$ maximal constant runs. In a block of
length $L$ with signature $(a,c)$, define
\[
 U(r)=(2a+c)r-a\operatorname{popcount}(r).
\]
The sum of $\nu_2(t)$ through $r$ is $r-\operatorname{popcount}(r)$.
Consequently every feasible block increasing the skeleton count from
$r_0$ to $r_1$ earns exactly $U(r_1)-U(r_0)$. Its internal arrangement
affects only feasibility, not reward.

Let $t$ be the previous key's bit and $u$ the final bit of a nonempty
block. Its possible number of ones is every integer in $[\ell,b]$:
\[
\begin{array}{cc|cc}
t&u&\ell&b\\\hline
1&1&\lceil L/2\rceil&L\\
0&1&\lfloor L/2\rfloor+1&L\\
1&0&\lfloor L/2\rfloor&L-1\\
0&0&\lceil L/2\rceil&L-1
\end{array}
\]
Empty ranges are infeasible. These bounds follow by separating zeros
with ones, respecting the first and last bits; every intermediate count
is attained. Use virtual initial bit $t=1$ and either final bit.

\subsection{Sliding transfers and linear-space reconstruction}
For a prefix reward profile $D_t(r_0)$, the exact next profile is
\begin{equation}
 D'_u(r)=U(r)+\max_{t\in\{0,1\}}
       \max_{r-b\le r_0\le r-\ell}\{D_t(r_0)-U(r_0)\}.
 \label{eq:run-transfer}
\end{equation}
Bounds $\ell,b$ depend on $t,u$. Exclude unreachable and out-of-range
entries. The inner maximum is a sliding window; a standard monotone
deque evaluates all ranks in linear time. Each candidate is inserted
and removed at most once. There are four bit pairs. Backward profiles
use the analogous window over $r_1\in[r+\ell,r+b]$ with values
$B'_u(r_1)+U(r_1)$, then subtract $U(r)$. Popcounts are tabulated once.

\begin{theorem}[Exact joint-signature optimizer]
For arbitrary nonnegative mixed weights, let $k$ be the number of runs
of $(p_i,q_i+q_{i+1})$. Exact optimization over all minimum-path-length
shapes, including full reconstruction, takes $O(nk)$ arithmetic operations
and $O(n)$ auxiliary space for $n\ge1$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
An initial sweep finds the maximum final reward at rank $m$. For traceback,
split the block list in half and compute its left forward and right
backward profiles between fixed endpoint ranks and bits. Their sum selects
an optimal middle state. Release both profiles before recursively solving
the two halves. A one-block leaf explicitly places its required count of
nonadjacent zeros using the boundary rules. The complete word yields all
comparison lengths and the existing linear Cartesian reconstruction.
This uses Hirschberg's established recomputation principle~\cite{hirschberg}.

At recursion level $d$, an active subproblem has at most
$\lceil k/2^d\rceil$ blocks. The sum of active rank-interval widths is
at most $m+2^d$, because endpoint rank spans partition the original span.
Summing sweep work gives $O(km+k\log(k+1))$, plus $O(n)$ preprocessing
and output. Since $m=\Theta(n)$ and $k\le n$, this is $O(nk)$.
Only a constant number of profiles is retained at once; releasing them
before recursion prevents a stack of profiles. All other summaries and
the output occupy $O(n)$ space.
\end{proof}

A perfect shape or a single signature block is constructed directly.
The potential and transfer use arbitrary block values; they do not assume
that an affine approximation is accurate or that the reference tree stays
optimal.

\subsection{Certification and full mixed-weight guarantees}
The mixed residual-Huffman gate remains
\[
 C_*\le(a_0+b_0)(I_{\min}(n)+1)+(a_0+2b_0)n+H,
 \qquad a_0=\min p,\quad b_0=\min q.
\]
It can also be built from the compressed signatures. Each block contains
one key weight. Its gap weights obey $q_{i+1}=c-q_i$, so they alternate
between at most two values. Record the block's $L$ keys as one weighted
multiplicity and its $L$ left gaps as at most two; finally add $q_n$ once.
There are at most $k$ key records and $2k+1$ gap records. Sort these
records, expand their multiplicities, and run the two-queue bound. The
total work is $O(k\log(k+1)+n)$ and space is $O(n)$. Gaps at block
boundaries occur once. Thus an accepted full solve retains the theorem's
time and space bounds. Failed gates use exact Knuth; the fixed-profile
preflight rejection also remains available.

\begin{theorem}[Arbitrary mixed block values above a baseline]
For $n\ge1$, $M\ge1$, $0\le u_i,v_i\le M$, set
\[
 B=4(n+1)^2M,\qquad p_i=B+u_i,\qquad q_i=B+v_i.
\]
If $(u_i,v_i+v_{i+1})$ has $k$ runs, the standalone signature constructor
returns a full optimal mixed-weight BST in $O(nk)$ arithmetic operations
and $O(n)$ auxiliary space. Constant $k$ gives linear construction and
$k=o(n)$ gives subquadratic construction.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The residual mass above $a_0,b_0$ is at most $(2n+1)M$. Every class
candidate has residual cost at most $(h+1)(2n+1)M<2B\le a_0+b_0$.
A tree outside the class pays an additional baseline cost of at least
$a_0+b_0$. Hence the gate passes even with $H=0$, and preflight cannot
reject. The exact class optimum is a full optimum. The preceding theorem
and compressed certificate give the stated work and space.
\end{proof}

The weights are positive and both types may be nonconstant. Key values
can change arbitrarily between blocks, and gap weights can alternate at
every position. No small perturbation budget or affine-closeness assumption
is imposed on $u,v$. If $M$ is polynomial in $n$, all weights and costs
have $O(\log n)$ bits, giving the corresponding word-RAM bound. For
arbitrarily large $M$, bit costs remain additional. The large baseline
is a real restriction; few signatures alone do not guarantee the full gate.
For example, $p=(100,1,1,1)$ and $q=(1,1,1,1,1)$ have only two signatures,
but their best minimum-path-length shape costs 218 and their full optimum
is 121. Direct enumeration of all fourteen trees, cubic DP, and Knuth agree;
the separate gate correctly rejects the class candidate.

\subsection{Bounded routing and its space qualification}

\section{Global alternating-gap bounds and linear profile construction}
The preceding signature representation can have $k=n$ even on a short
alternating pattern. We now avoid its rank table by recognizing attainment
of a global key-depth lower bound. The crucial gap inequality removes the
large-baseline restriction on the recognized family.

\subsection{A gap lower bound valid for all tree shapes}
For $n\ge1$, set $h=\lfloor\log_2(n+1)\rfloor$, $S=2^h$, and
$s=n+1-S$. Suppose $q_i$ alternates between arbitrary nonnegative values
$a,b$. Every minimum-internal-path-length shape has gap cost
\[
 G(q)=h\sum_{i=0}^n q_i+s(a+b).
\]
\begin{lemma}[Alternating-gap component bound]
Every BST on these keys, including every taller tree, has gap contribution
at least $G(q)$. Every minimum-internal-path-length shape attains equality.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix one parity of the $n+1$ inorder gaps. Give its $H$ leaves unit weight
and the other $Z=n+1-H$ leaves zero weight. Delete a zero leaf and contract
its parent into its sibling. The sibling contains a unit-weight leaf:
remaining zero leaves are separated in inorder by unit leaves, and a
subtree's leaves are contiguous. An all-zero sibling would contradict
that separation. Deleting only zero leaves preserves separation.
Each contraction decreases the selected depth sum by at least one.
After all $Z$ contractions, a full tree with $H$ unit leaves remains.
Its external path length is at least $E_{\min}(H)$.

Here $S/2\le H\le S$, so the standard balanced-leaf formula gives
$E_{\min}(H)=(h+1)H-S$, valid at both endpoints. Thus the original selected
depth sum is at least
\[
 E_{\min}(H)+Z=(h+1)H-S+n+1-H=hH+s.
\]
A minimum-path-length shape has gaps at depths $h,h+1$. Each of its $s$
bottom cherries has two adjacent gaps, one of each parity, at depth $h+1$.
Thus its selected depth sum is exactly $hH+s$. Apply the inequality to
both parities and multiply by the nonnegative weights $a,b$.
\end{proof}

Let $p_{(1)}\ge\cdots\ge p_{(n)}$ be the key weights sorted by value,
ignoring their fixed positions only for a lower bound. There are at most
$2^{d-1}$ keys at comparison length $d$. Consequently the sorted lengths
of every BST dominate $\operatorname{bitlength}(1),\ldots,
\operatorname{bitlength}(n)$ componentwise. Rearrangement yields the
globally valid bound
\[
 K(p)=\sum_{r=1}^n p_{(r)}\operatorname{bitlength}(r),\qquad
 \OPT(p,q)\ge K(p)+G(q).                                      \tag{28}
\]
An ordered tree attaining both components is globally optimal. This
compares with all shapes; it does not assume that an optimum is nearly
complete. Finding a tree attaining $K(p)$ is the remaining constructive task.

\subsection{Two key levels reduce to interlaced intervals}
Suppose key weights have two distinct levels $\ell<u$, and let $H$ count
the high keys. Put $m=S-1$ and assume $S/2-1\le H\le m$.
In a minimum-path-length shape, even skeleton ranks have comparison length
less than $h$, odd skeleton ranks have length $h$, and cherries have length
$h+1$. Attainment of $K(p)$ is therefore equivalent to retaining every high
key in the skeleton and placing high keys at every even skeleton rank.
Low keys may occupy odd ranks or cherries. The inorder word must have $m$
ones and no adjacent zeros, as in the preceding section.

Read the high/low pattern from left to right. A high key must be retained;
a low key can be retained only when its new rank is odd. At a boundary
immediately after a high key, the previous bit is one. Encode reachable
even ranks as $2A$ and odd ranks as $2B+1$. The virtual initial boundary
has $A=\{0\}$, $B=\varnothing$.

\begin{lemma}[Interlaced parity intervals]
The sets $A,B$ are integer intervals, possibly empty. When both are
nonempty, their lower endpoint difference and their upper endpoint
difference each belong to $\{0,1\}$. Four endpoints and empty flags thus
represent the exact reachable state.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $L$ low keys precede the next high key. The exact transformations are
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
L&A'&B'\\\hline
0&B+1&A\\
1&(A\cup B)+1&A\\
2\text{ or }3&A+1&\varnothing\\
\ge4&\varnothing&\varnothing
\end{array}
\]
For $L=1$, remove the low key or retain it at an odd rank. For $L=2$,
the low word is $01$ or $10$; for $L=3$ it is $010$. These require an
even incoming rank. A low-only run can retain at most one key, because
two retained lows would occupy consecutive skeleton ranks, one even.
At most one removed key can lie on either side, so $L\ge4$ is impossible.

For $L=0$, each endpoint difference becomes one minus its previous value.
For $L=1$ with both intervals nonempty, their union is
$[\min B,\max A]$. The new lower difference is
$1+\min B-\min A\in\{0,1\}$ and the upper difference is one.
If only $A$ is nonempty, the pair becomes $(A+1,A)$; if only $B$ is
nonempty, only $B+1$ survives as $A'$. The remaining cases are immediate.
This proves the invariant inductively.
\end{proof}

For a terminal low run, write $R$ for all incoming ranks and $E$ for its
even subset. The final ranks are $R$ for $L=0$, $R\cup(E+1)$ for $L=1$,
$E+1$ for $L=2,3$, and empty otherwise. Save each high boundary's intervals.
After selecting final rank $m$, trace backward: each step has at most two
candidate predecessor ranks, and the short words above realize the chosen
transition. Concatenating the words and applying the comparison-length
Cartesian reconstruction returns the full tree in linear time and space.
Parity cannot be discarded: the high-key pattern $010100$ has reachable
final ranks $\{3,5\}$, so a single ordinary interval would invent rank four.

\begin{theorem}[Full linear construction on attaining two-level patterns]
For arbitrary nonnegative two-level key weights with $S/2-1\le H\le S-1$
and arbitrary nonnegative alternating gap weights, the recognizer decides
whether a minimum-path-length shape attains the stated key profile and
constructs a witness in
$O(n)$ arithmetic operations and $O(n)$ auxiliary space. Every accepted
witness is a full optimal mixed-weight BST, without a baseline assumption.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The interval transitions and traceback take constant work per high boundary
and linear total input/output work. Only two key multiplicity records are
ordered, so computing $K(p)$ also takes linear work. The recognized word
assigns the high keys to the shortest $H$ comparison lengths, attaining
$K(p)$. Its minimum-path-length shape attains $G(q)$. Equality in (28)
certifies global optimality, including comparison with every taller tree.
\end{proof}

One key level is handled directly. At perfect sizes $s=0$, the implementation
constructs and evaluates the unique class shape. Equality in (28) certifies
it; otherwise the earlier mixed-Huffman gate may certify it. Failure invokes
exact Knuth. Rejected recognition does not imply that every minimum-path-length
shape is suboptimal. The standalone solver and routed portfolio both remain
exact, with quadratic worst-case fallback. The linear-space guarantee applies
to the successful route; general fallback can use quadratic space.

\subsection{An explicit alternating family with no baseline}
\begin{corollary}[Alternating keys and gaps]
For $n\ge1$ with $n+1$ not a power of two, strictly alternating two-level
nonnegative key weights and alternating nonnegative gap weights admit full
exact $O(n)$-time, $O(n)$-space construction. The relative weight magnitudes
are unrestricted. Equal key levels are handled by the uniform route.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Here $s>0$. Let $A=1$ if the first key is high and $A=0$ otherwise.
If $s\bmod2=A$, remove the first $s$ low keys. Otherwise remove the first
$s-1$ lows and the last low. There are enough lows, and their positions
are nonadjacent. Every high key remains. Before the initially removed lows
end, retained keys are high. After removing $t$ initial lows, a high key's
skeleton-rank parity is $A-t$. The suffix therefore has high keys at even
ranks when $t\bmod2=A$. In the second construction, removing the final low
affects at most one subsequent high, which may occupy an odd rank.
Every even skeleton rank is high. The high count lies in the theorem's
range, so the recognizer succeeds and (28) proves the full claim.
\end{proof}

This family has $k=n$ joint signatures when the key levels differ. The
interval representation therefore escapes the preceding $O(nk)$ bound
without invoking a small-residual assumption. Nonperiodic accepted patterns
are also possible: choose any minimum-path-length shape, mark all even
skeleton ranks high, choose the odd skeleton labels freely, and label every
cherry low. Recognition receives only the resulting weights, not this witness.

The perfect-size exception matters. For $p=(100,1,100)$ and four zero gaps,
the unique minimum-path-length tree costs 401 while the full optimum is 303.
Cubic DP and Knuth agree. Thus the corollary cannot include every size and
orientation. Arbitrary integer magnitudes also retain their bit costs;
polynomially bounded weights yield the corresponding word-RAM bounds.

Zeros, ties, huge integers, random, geometric, near-uniform, arbitrary
binary patterns with and without a baseline, and earlier affine inputs
are included.

Both scaling families now have no added baseline. At 393,216 keys,
a nonperiodic witness-generated pattern has 262,111 joint-signature runs
but only 196,305 interval transitions; strict alternation has 393,216 runs
and 196,608 transitions. They require 212,793 and 262,144 membership
queries respectively, and one traceback step per stored high boundary.
Each orders two multiplicity records, expands 393,216 key weights,
constructs every key, and directly checks the full mixed cost. Neither
uses a residual-Huffman contraction or a dense shape state. These are
operation categories, not machine-instruction totals or measured peak bytes.
Ten scaling portfolio controls agree; the two 1536-key cases also match
Knuth and the unrestricted shape DP. Larger cases use the proved global
bound and direct output-cost verification.

an unrestricted subquadratic algorithm. The global gap lemma, interval
invariant, and full recognized-family guarantee do not depend on the timings.

\section{Status and reproducibility}
The general subquadratic question remains open. This checkpoint provides
exact contraction, bounded-excess and split-height constructions, variable
interval frontiers, dominant-gap separators, joint heavy-outcome cores,
residual exclusion and coupled two-gap skeleton certificates, global certificates,
scoped obstructions to depth and endpoint-response representations, and
a core-sparse product reduction for integer-mass inputs with verified
condensed constructors and measured implementation regressions, and a
centered core bound independent of large baseline magnitudes in the
arithmetic operation count.
An exchange certificate now avoids wide last-level shape DPs on accepted
inputs. A positive ramp family proves a quadratic support barrier for
explicit Monge cores and their fourth differences, while admitting exact
logarithmic-time interval queries and linear construction.
Affine-regret corridors extend exact construction to arbitrary signed
perturbations of that family, with $O(n\log^2 n)$ work under a total-magnitude
bound and $O(n\log n)$ under independent unit perturbations.
Joint key/adjacent-gap signatures give an exact $O(nk)$ minimum-path-length
optimizer with linear-space reconstruction and a fully certified positive
family with arbitrary block values.
A global alternating-gap lower bound now combines with interlaced parity
intervals to give linear full construction on recognized two-level patterns
without a large baseline, including nonperfect alternating inputs.
Mathematical novelty, broad
practical benefit, and comparisons against other optimized prior implementations
remain to be assessed.

Standard-library Python and C++ code, seeded exact results, and matched timing
observations are under \texttt{obst/code} and \texttt{obst/results}.
The README gives reproduction commands. The active objective remains to
avoid most of the quadratic table on a substantially broader class, or
establish a rigorously scoped complexity lower bound.
